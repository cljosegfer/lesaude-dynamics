\subsection{Pairing system}
\label{sec:pairing-appendix}

Longitudinal pairs $(\mX_t, \mX_{t+1})$ for pretraining are constructed directly from MIMIC-IV-ECG's hospital-stay structure, using \texttt{ecg\_no\_within\_stay} (enws) as the per-admission ECG counter and \texttt{subject\_id} to group all ECGs belonging to the same patient, time-ordered by \texttt{ecg\_time}. Two pair types are distinguished:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Within-stay}: consecutive ECGs recorded during the same hospital stay, i.e.\ enws increasing between two chronologically adjacent records for a patient. Since ICD-10 diagnosis codes in this dataset are assigned at the hospitalization level (one code set per admission), $\vy_t = \vy_{t+1}$ for essentially every within-stay pair; the transition $\va_t = \text{clip}(\vy_{t+1}-\vy_t,-1,1)$ is almost always the zero vector, so this pair type contributes encoder training signal from waveform variability alone, not from a genuine diagnostic transition.
\item \textbf{Cross-stay}: the first ECG of stay $N$ paired with the first ECG of stay $N+1$ for the same patient (enws $=0$ on both sides), i.e.\ pairing across separate admissions rather than within one. Because separate hospitalizations frequently carry different discharge diagnoses, this is where the pathology-transition signal $\va_t \ne \vec{0}$ actually concentrates.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats} reports, on the training split (folds 0--17, 721{,}002 exams), the pair count, the stable/active split (stable: $\va_t=\vec{0}$; active: $\lVert\va_t\rVert_0>0$), and the mean number of flipped ICD-10 columns among active pairs, for each pair type. As expected from the per-admission label assignment, within-stay pairs are stable in 160{,}371/160{,}400 ($99.98\%$) of cases; cross-stay pairs are close to evenly split (72{,}386 stable / 77{,}537 active), confirming that admission-to-admission transitions are the primary source of diagnostic-change supervision for the dynamics objective.

\paragraph{Multistep cross-stay pairing.} The cross-stay pairing above only links stay $N$ to stay $N+1$ (hop distance $k=1$). We additionally explore pairing stay $N$ to stay $N+k$ for growing $k$, pooling all hops up to $k$ into a single, larger cross-stay pair set (all such pairs stay within one patient, so pooling introduces no fold leakage). For a patient with $L$ recorded stays, hop $k$ contributes $\max(0, L-k)$ pairs; across the 111{,}573 training patients with at least one stay (mean 2.34, median 1, max 125 stays per patient), $k=1$ alone reproduces the 149{,}923-pair count of Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats} exactly, which we use as a consistency check between the two pair-construction scripts.

\subsection{Pairing Scheme Ablation: Pretraining Dynamics}
\label{sec:pairing-ablation}

We compare three pretraining configurations of the forward Dynamics model (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}), differing only in which pairs the SSL objective is trained on: (i) the pooled baseline, using both within-stay and cross-stay pairs from the traditional pair index ($k=1$ only); (ii) cross-stay-only, restricting the same traditional pair index to cross-stay pairs; and (iii) multistep cross-stay, pooling cross-stay pairs at every hop $k=1,\dots,12$ (Section~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix}). All three share the same encoder architecture, batch size, and optimizer configuration; only \texttt{pairs\_path} and \texttt{pair\_types} differ.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{papel/fig/pairing_ablation_losses.png}
\caption{Validation loss curves for the three pairing configurations of Section~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation}: SIGReg regularization loss (left), dynamics prediction loss (middle), and total loss (right), against training step.}
\label{fig:pairing-ablation-losses}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:pairing-ablation-losses} shows two effects not anticipated from encoder architecture alone. First, cross-stay-only pretraining reaches a visibly higher validation \texttt{pred\_loss} than the pooled baseline, consistent with training on substantially fewer pairs under the same epoch budget: pooling within-stay and cross-stay pairs yields 310{,}323 training pairs (Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats}), versus 149{,}923 for cross-stay alone, so the restricted run sees under half the unique pairs per epoch and overfits sooner despite within-stay pairs being label-stable ($99.98\%$) and thus contributing mostly waveform-level, rather than transition-level, training signal. Second, the multistep run's \texttt{reg\_loss} sits roughly $5\times$ higher than the baseline's. The SIGReg statistic (Appendix~\ref{sec:sigreg} % TODO: fix ref if the SIGReg formula is documented under a different label
) is a sliced Epps--Pulley goodness-of-fit statistic that is $O(1)$ under a well-matched (near-Gaussian) embedding distribution but scales linearly with the effective batch size used in its estimator under any sustained deviation from it; since $\lambda$ was calibrated against the baseline configuration's regularizer scale, a shift in that scale — whether from genuinely harder-to-regularize embeddings under the larger, more heterogeneous multistep pair pool, or from an unpinned world-size/batch-scale confound across runs — changes the effective SSL/regularization balance without $\lambda$ itself having been retuned. %TODO: confirm via train/loss_ratio logs and matched world_size across runs before drawing a final conclusion here; update this paragraph once confirmed.

\subsection{Embedding-Space Diagnostic}
\label{sec:embedding-diagnostic}

To probe what each of the three pretrained Dynamics checkpoints from Section~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation} has actually learned beyond the pretraining loss curves, we run a post-hoc diagnostic on a random batch of validation pairs. For each pair, we compute the actual current- and next-state embeddings $\vh_t=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_t)$, $\vh_{t+1}=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_{t+1})$, and three candidate predictions of $\vh_{t+1}$ from $\vh_t$: $\hat{\vh}_{t+1}=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ using the pair's true transition; a zero-action baseline $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\vec{0}))$; and a noise-action baseline $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\tilde{\va}_t))$ using a synthetic ternary vector $\tilde{\va}_t$ magnitude-matched to the pair's own true action — $\lVert\tilde{\va}_t\rVert_0=\lVert\va_t\rVert_0$, with flips placed on uniformly random label positions and random $\pm1$ signs — i.e.\ ``right sparsity, wrong labels,'' isolating whether $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ uses \emph{which} labels changed rather than merely how many. We score all three against $\vh_{t+1}$ by MSE and by cosine similarity, both broken down by transition magnitude $\lVert\va_t\rVert_0$ (the number of ICD-10 columns that flip) into all pairs, active pairs ($\lVert\va_t\rVert_0>0$), and stable pairs ($\va_t=\vec{0}$), to test whether each checkpoint's predictions specifically track the size of the diagnostic change rather than being uniformly accurate or uniformly insensitive to $\va_t$.

\subsection{Displacement-Conditioned Dynamics Variant}
\label{sec:displacement-diagnostic}

The Inverse Dynamics model (Section~\ref{sec:inverse}) is parameterized in terms of the latent displacement $\vd_t = \vh_{t+1}-\vh_t$ rather than the absolute next state. We implement the analogous reparameterization for the forward Dynamics model: $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ is trained to output a predicted displacement $\hat{\vd}_t=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ rather than a predicted next state, and the prediction term of the loss is computed directly against the true displacement,
\[
\mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda)\,\text{MSE}\big(\vh_{t+1}-\vh_t,\ \text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))\big) + \lambda\,\big(\text{SIGReg}(H_t)+\text{SIGReg}(H_{t+1})\big),
\]
with the architecture of $\text{Dyn}_\phi$, the SIGReg regularization, and all other hyperparameters (batch size, optimizer, $\lambda$, learning rate) unchanged from the standard next-state formulation of Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}, so that the two variants differ only in what the predictor's output is trained to represent.

To probe this variant with the same methodology as Section~\ref{sec:embedding-diagnostic}, we reuse its do-nothing / true-action / zero-action / noise-action comparison, reconstructing a next-state prediction for each condition as $\vh_t+\hat{\vd}_t$ so that all four columns of Table~\ref{tab:embedding-mse-displacement} remain directly comparable, in units of MSE to $\vh_{t+1}$, to the corresponding columns of Table~\ref{tab:embedding-mse}.

\subsection{CEM Planning with the Forward Dynamics Model}
\label{sec:cem-planning}

This section evaluates the Planning procedure of Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}, in which diagnosis is recovered by searching for the transition $\va^\star$ that the forward model $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ judges to best explain an observed pair of embeddings. We report it in full because the outcome is negative, and because the way it fails is informative about what the forward model has, and has not, learned.

\paragraph{Setup.} The search follows Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}: two Bernoulli heads, onset and resolution, masked by the known previous label $\vy_{t-1}$ so that only admissible transitions are proposed. Both are initialized at Laplace-smoothed per-class base rates estimated on the 149{,}923 training cross-stay pairs. The onset rate has mean $0.0104$ and median $0.0028$; the resolution rate has mean $0.476$ and median $0.476$. Each CEM run uses a population of $N=512$ candidates, $K=64$ elites and $16$ iterations. The elite refit keeps weight $0.1$ on the previous distribution. Every Bernoulli parameter is clamped to $[0.005, 0.995]$ so that rare onsets remain explorable. The do-nothing action seeds the incumbent, so $E(\va^\star)\le E(\vec 0)$ by construction. We verified the search on a synthetic oracle energy $E(\va)=\lVert\va-\va_{\text{gt}}\rVert^2$: it recovers the true action exactly for every pair, and all $2.1$M sampled candidates were admissible.

We evaluate all seven forward checkpoints: the three pairing configurations of Section~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation}, the displacement variant of Section~\ref{sec:displacement-diagnostic}, and, for the three next-state configurations, the twin checkpoint selected by the online linear-probe AUROC rather than by validation loss. Evaluation uses exactly the protocol of the inverse model and the carry-forward baseline: the 8{,}062 cross-stay test pairs ($k=1$, fold 19), macro AUROC over classes with both outcomes present, and 1{,}000 bootstrap resamples with a fixed seed. Onset and resolution AUROCs are computed only over the labels where each head is live ($\vy_{t-1,i}=0$ and $\vy_{t-1,i}=1$ respectively). Scoring them over all entries would credit the planner with the structural knowledge that a present label cannot have an onset.

\paragraph{Reference numbers.} Recomputed on the current version of the pair index, the carry-forward baseline $\hat{\vy}_t=\vy_{t-1}$ reaches $0.7515$ $[0.7361$--$0.7682]$ and the inverse dynamics model reaches $0.8286$ $[0.8124$--$0.8456]$ contextualized macro AUROC.
The inverse model's (unmasked) onset and resolution AUROCs are $0.6591$ and $0.6958$.

\paragraph{Minimizing reconstruction error alone.} Table~\ref{tab:cem-test} reports the planner with candidates ranked by $E(\va)$ alone. For every checkpoint, the contextualized AUROC falls far below carry-forward. The soft readout is at or near chance ($0.45$--$0.55$), and the hard readout reaches only $0.57$--$0.61$.

The diagnostics isolate the cause in the scoring function, not in the search:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The true action carries no signal.} The ground-truth transition beats the do-nothing action in only $47$--$54\%$ of active pairs, where chance is $50\%$. When it does, the mean reduction in $E$ is at most $0.005$ (Table~\ref{tab:cem-test}). This reproduces, on the full test set and for all seven checkpoints, the earlier finding on a single checkpoint that raw reconstruction error does not favour the true action on held-out data.
\item \textbf{The search exploits the model.} CEM lowers $E$ by $40$--$75\%$ relative to the do-nothing action. It does so with $6$--$12$ onsets per pair, against $0.67$ in the ground truth, and onset precision stays below $2\%$.
\item \textbf{Every active pair is a model failure.} In every active pair, the returned action scores at least as well as the true one, and it is never the true one. The search is not missing the true action; the model assigns lower error to transitions that never occur.
\end{itemize}
In other words, $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ is \emph{controllable} but not \emph{identifiable}: the $2^C$ admissible action codes, most far outside the training distribution of $\approx 2.5$ flips per active pair, span enough of the displacement space to fit a large part of $\vh_t-\vh_{t-1}$, while the one realistic code, the true transition, fits no better than doing nothing.

\paragraph{Prior-regularized objective.} Ranking candidates by $F(\va)=E(\va)/\tau+\sum_{i:\va_i\ne0}\log\frac{1-\pi_i}{\pi_i}$ instead (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}) makes every flip pay its prior log-odds: about $4.6$--$5.9$ nats for a typical onset, and close to zero, or negative, for a resolution. We swept $\tau\in\{3{\cdot}10^{-4},\dots,1\}$ on the validation split (8{,}244 cross-stay pairs, 100 bootstrap resamples) for all seven checkpoints (Table~\ref{tab:cem-tau}).

The prior removes the exploitation: onsets per pair fall from $8$--$12$ at $\tau\to0$ to $\approx0.3$ at $\tau=10^{-2}$, and to zero for $\tau\ge0.3$. The best soft AUROC improves accordingly, from $0.46$--$0.54$ to $0.57$--$0.59$, but it remains far below validation carry-forward ($0.758$). The hard readout peaks at $0.675$. At no temperature do the masked onset or resolution AUROCs leave the $0.50$--$0.53$ band, so the regularization suppresses spurious transitions without uncovering a real signal. This is consistent with the headroom test above, where the true transition does not lower $E$ in the first place.

\paragraph{Why the soft readout falls short of carry-forward even at large $\tau$.} As $\tau\to\infty$, $F$ reduces to the prior, whose posterior marginals are exactly the base rates. Reading those marginals through the contextualized combination gives $\vy_{t-1,i}(1-\pi^{\text{res}}_i)+(1-\vy_{t-1,i})\pi^{\text{ons}}_i$, a score that preserves the carry-forward ordering and so matches its AUROC.

CEM's final head parameters are not posterior marginals, however. The elite refit concentrates each Bernoulli on the mode, so every label whose resolution rate exceeds $0.5$ collapses to $p^{\text{res}}_i\approx1$. Its persistence score then falls to $\approx0.005$, tied with the onset scores of absent labels, and the carry-forward ordering is lost ($0.58$ instead of $0.758$). The hard readout suffers the same loss for the same reason: resolving every label whose prior favours resolution scores $0.669$.

A readout based on per-label conditional posteriors around the returned action, $p_i=\sigma\big(F(\va^\star_{i\to0})-F(\va^\star_{i\to\text{flip}})\big)$, would recover carry-forward in the prior limit and add the model's evidence at finite $\tau$. We leave it to future work. Given the headroom results above, we expect it to match, not exceed, carry-forward with the current checkpoints.

\paragraph{Summary.} With the present forward models, CEM planning does not recover diagnostic transitions. Minimizing reconstruction error alone is exploited through out-of-distribution action combinations, and adding the prior removes the exploitation without uncovering signal, because the ground-truth transition is no better than doing nothing under $\text{Dyn}_\phi$. The inverse dynamics model, trained to discriminate transitions directly, reaches $0.8286$ on the same pairs.

Making the forward model usable as a planner likely requires a training objective that explicitly penalizes wrong-action predictions, for example a contrastive term separating $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ from predictions under decoy actions. Besides the posterior-marginal readout, this is the main direction we leave for future work.

\subsection{Joint Forward and Inverse Pretraining}
\label{sec:joint-pretraining}

The two world-model components have complementary strengths. The forward Dynamics encoder transfers best under finetuning (Table~\ref{tab:classification}), but its forward model cannot be used for planning (Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}). The Inverse Dynamics model gives the best contextualized inference (Table~\ref{tab:contextualized}), with a weaker encoder. This section tests whether a single encoder trained under both objectives obtains both.

\paragraph{Setup.} The forward predictor $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ (with its projector $\text{Proj}_\omega$) and the inverse predictor $\text{Inv}_\psi$ share the encoder $\text{Enc}_\theta$ and nothing else; there is no coupling term between them. With $\vh_t=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_t)$ and $\vh_{t+1}=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_{t+1})$, the loss adds the prediction terms of Sections~\ref{sec:dynamics} and~\ref{sec:inverse} with equal weight,
\[
\mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda)\Big(\text{MSE}\big(\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t)),\,\vh_{t+1}\big) + \text{BCE}^{\text{ons}}_t + \text{BCE}^{\text{res}}_t\Big) + \lambda\big(\text{SIGReg}(\mH_t)+\text{SIGReg}(\mH_{t+1})\big),
\]
where $\text{BCE}^{\text{ons}}_t$ and $\text{BCE}^{\text{res}}_t$ are the onset and resolution terms of $\text{Inv}_\psi(\vh_{t+1}-\vh_t)$. Both terms are computed on every pair of the multistep cross-stay pool ($k\le12$, 605{,}533 training pairs; Appendix~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix}).

\subsection{Signal-Space Decoder}
\label{sec:decoder}

The diagnostics of Appendices~\ref{sec:embedding-diagnostic} and~\ref{sec:cem-planning} stay in latent space: both compare $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ with $\vh_{t+1}$ by squared error. Neither says whether $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ moves an embedding the way a diagnostic transition changes the ECG, for instance whether the onset of a conduction block reshapes the QRS complex.

We decode each estimate of $\vh_{t+1}$ and compare it with the real next ECG, and we decode the predictions under the true action and under control actions to see which of them reproduce the observed change. We write $\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\va)=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va))$, $\text{Dec}(\vh)$ for the decoded signal, and use the Standard forward checkpoint (within- and cross-stay pairs, selected by validation loss; Appendix~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation}).

\paragraph{Decoding targets.} The encoder pools over time, so $\vh$ does not record where the beats fall in the 10\,s window. On 8\,s crops of validation ECGs, shifting the crop by 20\,ms to 2\,s moves $\vh$ by only $0.3$--$0.8\%$ of the mean squared distance between two unrelated ECGs ($8$--$24\%$ for a randomly initialized encoder). A decoder of the raw $12\times5000$ strip would therefore learn an average over beat positions. We decode what $\vh$ can carry instead: the 12-lead median beat, from $0.4$\,s before to $0.6$\,s after the QRS complex ($12\times500$ samples at 500\,Hz, in the input's per-lead z-units), together with the heart rate and the RR irregularity (normalized RMSSD). Beats are extracted by detecting QRS complexes on the lead-summed squared slope of a 5--20\,Hz band-passed signal, re-aligning each beat to a first-pass median by cross-correlation ($\pm20$\,ms), and taking the per-lead median. $99.7\%$ of the 800{,}035 ECGs yield at least three complete beats.

\paragraph{Decoder.} An MLP ($256\to1024\to1024$, ReLU) with a beat head and a rhythm head is trained with squared error on the frozen, standardized embeddings of every ECG in folds 0--17 (718{,}922 ECGs). Its epoch is selected on fold 18 and scores are reported on fold 19. It explains $53\%$ of the variance of the test beats ($R^2=0.532$, against $0.719$ on the features of a randomly initialized encoder: pretraining discards beat detail) and decodes the heart rate with correlation $0.87$. Decoding $\vh_{t+1}$ instead of $\vh_t$ lowers the beat error to the next ECG by $21\%$ ($0.259$ vs.\ $0.329$ on the cross-stay test pairs), so the embeddings do carry the change between visits.

\paragraph{Counterfactual comparison.} Following the headroom tests of the planning analysis (Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}), which compare the true transition with the do-nothing action, we compare the decoded prediction under the true action $\va_t$ with two controls. The \emph{zero action}, $\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\vec 0))$, shows what the state pathway does alone. The \emph{decoy action} $\tilde{\va}_t$ is admissible given $\vy_t$ and has the same numbers of onsets and resolutions as $\va_t$, placed at random on labels that $\va_t$ leaves unchanged. A model that uses \emph{which} labels change should reproduce the observed transition under $\va_t$ and not under $\tilde{\va}_t$.

Beats are compared by shape rather than by squared error, because shrinking the old beat toward the population mean lowers the error without producing the new morphology. Let $\mB_t$ and $\mB_{t+1}$ be the real median beats of $\mX_t$ and $\mX_{t+1}$, and $\rho$ the correlation of per-lead mean-centred beats over the three leads where the beat changes most. The shape score
\[
s(\vb)=\rho(\vb,\mB_{t+1})-\rho(\vb,\mB_t)
\]
is positive when a beat $\vb$ looks more like the new beat than the old one; a flattened copy of $\mB_t$ still scores negative. We keep the active test pairs whose beat changes most (the $10\%$ with the lowest $\rho(\mB_t,\mB_{t+1})$, 410 pairs) and on which the decoder sees both morphologies ($s(\text{Dec}(\vh_t))\le-0.2$ and $s(\text{Dec}(\vh_{t+1}))\ge0.2$). This leaves 179 pairs, ranked by $s(\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\va_t)))$. Leads with fewer than ten distinct amplitude values in either beat are excluded, since z-scoring a nearly flat lead turns its quantization steps into spurious shape changes.

To quantify a counterfactual, we compare the effect of each action relative to the zero action, $\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\va))-\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\vec 0))$. The correlation between the decoy's effect and the true action's is $1$ when the model produces the same change whatever the labels, and should be low for an action-specific model.

\paragraph{Comparison with the zero action.} Figure~\ref{fig:decoder-top} shows the two highest-ranked pairs. In the first, the tall R waves of $\mX_t$ in aVL and V5 give way in $\mX_{t+1}$ to broad negative complexes in III and aVL and a small RS complex in V5; the action includes the onsets of acute myocardial infarction (I21) and of a conduction block (I44). The decoded $\vh_{t+1}$ shows the new complexes and the decoded $\vh_t$ keeps the old R waves. The decoded prediction follows the new morphology as closely as the ceiling: its correlation with $\mB_{t+1}$ and $\mB_t$ is $0.91$ and $-0.01$, against $0.91$ and $0.00$ for the decoded $\vh_{t+1}$. The zero action does not produce this change ($s=-0.33$, against $0.91$ under $\va_t$), so here the action moves the beat. In the second pair, by contrast, the zero action already reproduces the change ($s=0.84$): there it is the state pathway that moves the beat.

Such examples are rare. The decoded prediction looks more like $\mB_{t+1}$ than like $\mB_t$ in 10 of the 179 pairs ($6\%$), and the zero action in 4. In the median pair, the prediction closes $3\%$ of the gap in $s$ between the decoded $\vh_t$ and the decoded $\vh_{t+1}$, and in 53 pairs it moves away from $\mB_{t+1}$. Figure~\ref{fig:decoder-middle-bottom} shows the middle- and last-ranked pairs: the decoded $\vh_{t+1}$ reproduces the new morphology (a tall R wave in V5; a complete QRS inversion in III, aVL and aVF), while the decoded prediction lies on the decoded $\vh_t$. In squared error the prediction still beats do-nothing on $72\%$ of the 179 pairs, by shrinking the old beat rather than changing its shape.

\paragraph{Comparison with a decoy action.} Figures~\ref{fig:decoder-decoy-1} and~\ref{fig:decoder-decoy-2} add the decoy prediction to the four highest-ranked pairs.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An action-specific case.} In the third pair, the true action (onsets of cardiomyopathy I42, conduction block I44 and heart failure I50) inverts the QRS complexes of aVR, V5 and V6 toward $\mB_{t+1}$. The decoy (onsets I01, I66, I99) stays on the decoded $\vh_t$: its effect is $0.18\times$ the size of the true action's and correlates with it at $0.27$.
\item \textbf{A partly generic case.} In the first pair, the decoy shares no label with $\va_t$ but still reproduces part of the change in III and aVL, with an effect $0.54\times$ the size of the true action's and correlation $0.62$.
\item \textbf{Overall.} On the 2{,}724 active test pairs that admit a decoy, the decoy's effect is as large as the true action's (median ratio $1.08$) and correlates with it at a median of $0.39$ ($0.32$ over the 118 selected pairs that admit one). Among those 118 pairs, the decoy produces the new morphology in $2\%$, against $6\%$ for the true action over all 179.
\end{itemize}
$\text{Dyn}_\phi$ therefore moves the beat by about the same amount whatever labels change, partly along a direction shared by all transitions, with a small label-specific component. These pairs cannot tell whether a decoy's decoded change shows the features its own labels would cause, because no patient undergoes the decoy transition; that would require comparing with the change averaged over the pairs where those labels actually flip. Figure~\ref{fig:decoder-decoy-middle-bottom} repeats the middle and last pairs with the decoy: neither the true action nor the decoy produces the new morphology, and both predictions stay on the decoded $\vh_t$.

\paragraph{Summary.} The decoder recovers the change between visits from $\vh_{t+1}$, but the forward model rarely predicts it. Its improvement over do-nothing in latent space is accounted for by shrinkage toward the mean and does not yield a better decoded ECG. Isolated pairs show an action-driven change of morphology consistent with the transition, but in most pairs the prediction collapses onto the current state, $\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1})\approx\text{Dec}(\vh_t)$, and count-matched decoy actions move it as much as the true one.

\subsection{Encoder Architecture: ResNet vs.\ ViT}
\label{sec:cnn-vit}

The planning analysis (Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}) and the decoder (Appendix~\ref{sec:decoder}) show that $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ does not carry the effect of an action over to held-out transitions, although its training loss keeps falling. This section tests whether that failure is tied to the convolutional encoder. We replace $\text{Enc}_\theta$ with a small transformer and compare the two encoders on memorization of the training pairs, on the quality of their frozen features, and on planning.

\paragraph{Setup.} The transformer is a 1D ViT:
\begin{itemize}
\item Non-overlapping 50\,ms patches (25 samples at 500\,Hz) feed 4 pre-norm blocks of width 128 with 4 attention heads, rotary position embeddings, QK-normalization and SwiGLU MLPs.
\item Its tokens are mean-pooled and mapped linearly to $\vh_t\in\R^{256}$, as the ResNet's output layer does.
\item It has $0.87$M parameters, against $3.98$M for the ResNet; its size matches the ResNet's layers before its last stage ($0.96$M).
\end{itemize}

The two \emph{matched} runs differ only in the encoder. Both train on the multistep cross-stay pool ($k\le12$, Appendix~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix}) with $\lambda=0.05$, weight decay $0.1$, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 1{,}024 and a predictor of hidden width 512, for 100 epochs. Both are read at the final checkpoint, which no validation metric selected.

Secondary runs are not matched to each other and serve to show trends:
\begin{itemize}
\item The matched ResNet run at its best validation loss (epoch 5).
\item A ResNet trained on the multistep pool with $\lambda=0.005$ and weight decay $0.01$ (56 epochs).
\item A ResNet trained on the $k=1$ cross-stay pairs with $\lambda=0.005$ and weight decay $0.01$ (100 epochs).
\item The ViT trained on the $k=1$ cross-stay pairs with the matched recipe for 405 epochs, about as many batches as the multistep runs. It was run with predictor width 512 and with predictor width 128.
\end{itemize}

Randomly initialized ResNet and ViT encoders (seed 0) serve as controls.

\paragraph{Memorization test.} We use membership inference, with members and non-members drawn the same way.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Anchors.} For each patient, one stay $s$ that has a second ECG and a following stay gives three ECGs: $\mX_A$, the first ECG of stay $s$; $\mX_{A'}$, its second ECG; and $\mX_B$, the first ECG of stay $s+1$. The pair $A\to B$ is a $k=1$ cross-stay pair; $\mX_{A'}$ belongs to no cross-stay pair.
\item \textbf{Populations.} Members are 5{,}000 training patients and non-members all 2{,}518 patients of folds 18--19. Each patient contributes one anchor, so observations are independent.
\item \textbf{Membership is verified per checkpoint.} Every member's $A\to B$ pair is in the checkpoint's own training pairs, and no $\mX_{A'}$ and no non-member pair is.
\end{itemize}

Each test assigns one score per patient and reports the AUROC separating members from non-members, with DeLong 95\% confidence intervals. Chance is $0.5$, and the random-init encoders, which have no members, measure the floor. Distances are taken relative to the mean distance to other patients' $\vh_B$ in the same population.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pair:} closeness of $\vh_A$ to $\vh_B$. A high score means the recording and/or the patient is memorized.
\item \textbf{Patient:} closeness of the never-trained $\vh_{A'}$ to $\vh_B$. A high score means the patient is memorized.
\item \textbf{Recording:} the pair score relative to the patient score. A high score means the trained ECG sits closer to $\vh_B$ than an untrained ECG of the same stay, so the specific recording is memorized.
\item \textbf{Action:} on pairs with $\va_t\neq\vec 0$, the share of the prediction error removed by the true action, $1-E(\va_t)/E(\vec 0)$, where $E(\va)=\lVert\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_A,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va))-\vh_B\rVert^2$. A high score means the action effects of the training transitions are memorized in $\text{Dyn}_\phi$.
\end{itemize}

  \begin{itemize}

  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The ResNet's memorization follows exposure.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item On the multistep pool, the pair test rises from $0.867$ for ECGs in one training pair per epoch to $0.984$ for ECGs in $12.3$.

\item \textbf{The ViT encoder stays clean even under heavy repetition.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item After 405 epochs on $k=1$ pairs, in which each training pair is seen 405 times, its pair test is $0.518$.
  \item The only trace is in the most exposed patients of the multistep run, those with at least 9 stays: $0.566$ $[0.531$--$0.601]$.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The ViT's forward model memorizes instead.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The action test is $0.641$, as high as the ResNet's $0.633$. It reaches $0.832$ after 405 epochs on $k=1$ pairs, and a $3.8\times$ smaller predictor lowers it only to $0.766$.

  \item The smaller encoder moves the memorization into $\text{Dyn}_\phi$; it does not remove it.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item the 8{,}062 test pairs of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning} (fold 19);
\item the 8{,}271 pairs of training fold 17, which are in every checkpoint's training pairs (verified).
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CEM.} Ranking by $E$ alone, with the search and soft readout of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}.
\item \textbf{Decoys.} No search: the true action is ranked against two kinds of admissible decoy. \emph{Same-count} decoys are 16 actions with the true numbers of onsets and resolutions on random labels. The \emph{zero} decoy is the do-nothing action. The score is a within-pair AUROC, with chance at $0.5$.
\item \textbf{Per-label readout.} $\delta_i=E(\vec 0)-E(\ve_i)$ for the single admissible flip $\ve_i$ of label $i$, and $p_i=\sigma(\text{logit}\,\pi_i+\delta_i/\tau)$, combined as in the contextualized score. The temperature $\tau$ is chosen on fold 18. This is the readout anticipated at the end of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}.
\end{itemize}
Each decoy and per-label score is repeated with $\vh_t$ as the target in place of $\vh_{t+1}$ (``nothing changed''). This keeps the patient and removes the change, so what survives is read from $\vh_t$, not from the outcome ECG. Intervals come from a patient-level bootstrap. On these pairs carry-forward scores $0.750$ and the Inverse Dynamics model $0.827$ (point estimates).

\paragraph{Planning results} (Table~\ref{tab:cnn-vit-planning}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CEM fails with either encoder.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item On test, the soft readout scores $0.470$ for the ResNet and $0.519$ for the ViT, and $0.435$--$0.563$ across all checkpoints, against $0.750$ for carry-forward.

\item \textbf{On held-out pairs, the energy carries almost no evidence about the transition.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Against same-count decoys, the true action scores $0.524$ for the ResNet ($0.484$ when nothing changed) and $0.563$ for the ViT ($0.563$ when nothing changed). The ViT's higher score therefore comes from $\vh_t$ alone.
  \item The ViTs' true action is \emph{worse} than doing nothing on held-out pairs ($0.346$--$0.397$): their action effects move the prediction away from $\vh_{t+1}$.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{On training pairs, the energy carries strong evidence with either encoder.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Against same-count decoys, the two matched models score $0.722$ and $0.723$. After 100--405 epochs on $k=1$ pairs, the ResNet reaches $0.890$ and the ViT $0.849$.

  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The per-label readout beats carry-forward, but mostly through $\vh_t$.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item It scores $0.755$--$0.800$ on test, against $0.750$ for carry-forward.
  \item Scored against $\vh_t$ alone, it already reaches $0.740$--$0.782$. The outcome ECG adds at most $0.021$, and the same for both architectures: $+0.020$ each, paired difference $+0.000$ $[-0.023, +0.026]$.
  \item The validation AUROC is flat from about $3\times$ the spread of $\delta$ upward, which is the limit in which $E$ only breaks ties within each label's present and absent groups. Letting $E$ override the carry-forward ordering lowers it.
  \item The best readouts come from the least memorized forward models, the ResNet at epoch 5 ($0.800$) and Dynamics (Standard) ($0.790$). Both remain below the Inverse Dynamics model ($0.827$).
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Summary.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Encoder memorization.} Replacing the ResNet with the 4.6$\times$ smaller ViT removes the encoder-level memorization: trained pairs no longer collapse, and the dependence on exposure disappears.
\item \textbf{Forward-model memorization.} The swap does not make $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ generalize. The ViT's forward model memorizes the action effects of its training transitions as much as the ResNet's does.
\item \textbf{Frozen features.} The ViT does not give better features: the two encoders tie at $\vh$, and the ViT's middle-layer ceiling is lower.
\item \textbf{Planning.} Planning is unchanged. With either encoder, CEM is exploited, and the energy separates the true transition only on training pairs. A per-label readout gains about $0.02$ over carry-forward from the outcome ECG, the same for both. The planning failure lies in how the forward model generalizes, not in the encoder's collapse.
\item \textbf{Caveats.} The comparison does not yet separate architecture from capacity, since a ResNet of about 1M parameters or a ViT of about 4M has not been trained. Each configuration has one seed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pretraining Without Actions}
\label{sec:no-actions}

The forward objective conditions $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ on the transition $\va_t$, which is built from the labels. The preceding sections show that $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ does not carry the action's effect over to held-out pairs. This section asks whether the conditioning matters for the encoder at all. We retrain with every action set to $\vec 0$ and compare the finetuned encoders on MIMIC triage classification.

\paragraph{Setup.} We set $\va_t=\vec 0$ for every pair, in training and in validation. Because $\text{Proj}_\omega$ is an MLP with biases, $\text{Proj}_\omega(\vec 0)$ becomes a learned constant that the first layer of $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ absorbs as a bias. The model is therefore $\hat\vh_{t+1}=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t)$, with the same encoder, predictor capacity, SIGReg term and optimizer, and the first layer of $\text{Proj}_\omega$ receives no gradient. The objective no longer uses labels: it predicts the embedding of the patient's next stay from the current one. On the $k=1$ cross-stay pairs used here, this removes the action from the $51.7\%$ of training pairs that carry one, which change $2.48$ labels on average (Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats}).

Both runs use the ViT encoder of Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit} with its $k=1$ recipe: cross-stay pairs, $\lambda=0.05$, weight decay $0.1$, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 1{,}024, predictor width 512, seed 42 and 405 epochs. They differ only in $\va_t$. The action-conditioned run is the ``ViT, $k=1$, 405 epochs'' run of Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit}, and we also include its predictor-width-128 variant as a second action-conditioned run. Each run is read at its highest online linear-probe AUROC on validation, which falls at epoch 370 without actions and at epochs 402 and 403 with them.

Each encoder is then finetuned end to end with a linear head for the 76 labels on the 261{,}496 first-of-stay ECGs of the training folds, with learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$ and batch size 1{,}024. Training stops early on the loss over the 14{,}456 first-of-stay ECGs of fold 18 (patience 20), and we keep the checkpoint with the lowest loss (epochs 39, 57 and 57). The test set is the 14{,}237 first-of-stay ECGs of fold 19, scored as in Table~\ref{tab:classification}: macro-AUROC is the mean over 1{,}000 bootstrap resamples, reported with a 95\% interval.

Two quantities logged on the 8{,}244 validation pairs during pretraining complement the test. The \emph{error ratio} is the prediction error $\lVert\hat\vh_{t+1}-\vh_{t+1}\rVert^2$ relative to that of doing nothing, $\lVert\vh_t-\vh_{t+1}\rVert^2$, averaged over pairs. The prediction uses $\va=\vec 0$ without actions and the true $\va_t$ with them, and each model is measured in its own embedding space. The \emph{true vs.\ zero} score is the within-pair AUROC of Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit} of the true action against the zero action, on pairs with a change, under the action-conditioned model's own energy $E$. Chance is $0.5$, and a score below it means the zero action predicts $\vh_{t+1}$ better than the true one.

\subsection{Posterior Readout and Contrastive Training for Planning}
\label{sec:posterior-planning}

Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning} left two directions open for making the forward model usable as a planner. The first is to read per-label posterior marginals instead of the single action that CEM returns. The second is to train the energy with a term that penalizes predictions under decoy actions. This section tests both, separately and together, on the contextualized inference task of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}. We use the notation of Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit}: the planner observes $\vh_t$, $\vh_{t+1}$ and $\vy_t$, and scores admissible actions by $E(\va)=\lVert\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va))-\vh_{t+1}\rVert^2$, averaged over the embedding dimensions.

\paragraph{Why the search discards evidence.} A search returns one action per pair, while AUROC ranks a graded score per label, and the two diverge when transitions are rare and the evidence for each is weak. An inverse head refit on the frozen Inverse Dynamics features shows this directly. It has the architecture and BCE objective of $\text{Inv}_\psi$ (Section~\ref{sec:inverse}), and read through its own onset and resolution probabilities it reaches $0.828$ contextualized AUROC on the test pairs. Used as an energy, $E(\va)=-\log p(\va\mid\Delta\vh_t)$ is already a negative log-posterior, so the CEM search of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning} returns the posterior mode. That mode flips almost nothing ($0.006$ onsets per pair), and both CEM readouts score carry-forward to three decimals ($0.750$). The head's evidence is real, but it rarely pushes a label's flip probability above one half, and the mode keeps only what does. Without a prior term, the mode is replaced by the exploited minimum of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}. Either way, the information that AUROC ranks is lost before scoring.

\paragraph{Posterior readout.} Given $\vy_t$, each label has exactly one admissible flip: an onset if $\vy_{t,i}=0$ and a resolution if $\vy_{t,i}=1$. An admissible action is therefore a set of flipped labels, which we write as $\vz\in\{0,1\}^C$, with $\va=\vz\odot\vs$ and $\vs=\vec 1-2\vy_t$. The base-rate prior of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning} makes the flips independent, $p(\vz\mid\vy_t)=\prod_i\pi_i^{z_i}(1-\pi_i)^{1-z_i}$, where $\pi_i$ is the rate of label $i$'s live head. Treating $E$ as a log-likelihood with temperature $\tau$ gives the posterior
$$ p(\vz\mid\vh_t,\vh_{t+1},\vy_t)\;\propto\;p(\vz\mid\vy_t)\,\exp\!\big(-E(\vz\odot\vs)/\tau\big), $$
whose negative logarithm is the prior-regularized objective $F$ of Section~\ref{sec:dynamics} up to a constant. CEM searches for the minimum of $F$. The posterior readout instead computes each label's marginal probability of flipping, $q_i=P(z_i=1\mid\vh_t,\vh_{t+1},\vy_t)$, and scores the next state with the contextualized combination of Section~\ref{sec:inverse}, $\hat{\vy}_{t+1,i}=\vy_{t,i}(1-q_i)+(1-\vy_{t,i})\,q_i$.

The marginals are sums over $2^C$ actions, which we estimate in two strata. The first stratum is enumerated exactly. It holds the do-nothing action and the $C$ single flips $\ve_i$, with prior masses $p(\vec 0)=\prod_j(1-\pi_j)$ and $p(\ve_i)=p(\vec 0)\,\pi_i/(1-\pi_i)$, which on the test pairs hold $28.5\%$ and $35.3\%$ of the prior mass on average. The second stratum, actions with two or more flips, holds the remaining $36.2\%$. It is covered by $N=2{,}048$ draws $\vz^{(n)}$ from the prior, of which those with at least two flips enter with weight $1/N$. With $w(\va)=\exp\!\big(-(E(\va)-E(\vec 0))/\tau\big)$, where subtracting $E(\vec 0)$ only stabilizes the exponentials,
$$ q_i=\frac{p(\ve_i)\,w(\ve_i)+\frac1N\sum_{n:\,|\vz^{(n)}|\ge2} z^{(n)}_i\,w(\va^{(n)})}{p(\vec 0)\,w(\vec 0)+\sum_j p(\ve_j)\,w(\ve_j)+\frac1N\sum_{n:\,|\vz^{(n)}|\ge2} w(\va^{(n)})}. $$
The exact stratum matters because onsets are rare. With a median onset rate of $0.0028$, a typical label's onset appears in about six of the $2{,}048$ prior draws, so plain sampling would leave most labels' marginals noisy. Enumeration evaluates every label exactly where most of the mass lies. Each pair costs $2{,}125$ batched evaluations of $\text{Dyn}_\phi$, so a split of about 8{,}000 pairs takes seconds on one GPU. The temperature is chosen on the validation pairs (fold 18) from a log-spaced grid between $10^{-4}$ and $3$.

Restricting each label to the two candidates $\vec 0$ and $\ve_i$ reduces $q_i$ to $\sigma\big(\text{logit}\,\pi_i+(E(\vec 0)-E(\ve_i))/\tau\big)$, the per-label readout of Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit}. That readout is thus the posterior of a model in which each label competes only with doing nothing, and it never weighs multi-flip actions. The full posterior also lets labels compete with each other and with multi-flip explanations of the same change.

\paragraph{Energies.} We read eight forward energies with three readouts. The first is the forward model of Dynamics (Standard), its own $\text{Proj}_\omega$ and $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ trained end to end with the encoder, read on its raw embeddings. The other seven are heads of the same architecture (predictor width 512) trained in a second stage on frozen features: the $\vh$ of the Dynamics (Standard) encoder, the $\vh$ of the Inverse Dynamics encoder, and the pooled stage-3 features of a randomly initialized ResNet. Each feature set is standardized per dimension with training statistics. A head is trained for 20 epochs over the 149{,}923 training cross-stay pairs with AdamW (learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$, batch size 1{,}024). We keep the epoch with the best validation within-pair AUROC of the true action against same-count decoys (Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit}), which falls between epochs 3 and 18.

The MSE heads minimize $E(\va_t)$ alone. The InfoNCE heads add a cross-entropy over a candidate set $\mathcal{A}_t$ that contains the true action and a set of decoys,
$$ \mathcal{L}=E(\va_t)-\log\frac{\exp(-E(\va_t)/\kappa)}{\sum_{\va\in\mathcal{A}_t}\exp(-E(\va)/\kappa)}, $$
with a learned temperature $\kappa$ initialized at $0.05$, and with decoys identical to the true action masked out. \emph{Probe decoys} are 16 same-count decoys, 16 extra-flip decoys (the true action plus one admissible flip) and the do-nothing action. \emph{Prior decoys} are 32 actions drawn from the base-rate prior given $\vy_t$, the same distribution the posterior readout samples from. With negatives drawn from the prior, the optimum of the InfoNCE term satisfies $-E(\va)/\kappa=\log p(\va\mid\vh_t,\vh_{t+1},\vy_t)-\log p(\va\mid\vy_t)+c$, where $c$ is constant within a pair. Multiplying $\exp(-E/\kappa)$ by the prior then recovers the posterior, which is the quantity the readout computes.

\paragraph{Controls and references.} Every marginal readout is repeated with $\vh_t$ as the target in place of $\vh_{t+1}$ (``nothing changed''), at the temperature chosen on validation for the real target. What survives is read from $\vh_t$, so the gap between the two scores is the evidence that the outcome ECG contributes. CEM uses the settings of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}, ranking by $E$ alone. All scores are contextualized macro-AUROCs on the 8{,}062 test pairs, as point estimates from one training seed per head. On these pairs carry-forward scores $0.750$ and the Inverse Dynamics model $0.827$ (Table~\ref{tab:cnn-vit-planning}). Patient-level 95\% half-widths of contextualized scores on these pairs reach $0.028$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit}).

\paragraph{The readout recovers what the search discards.} On every energy, both marginal readouts score far above CEM (Table~\ref{tab:posterior-planning}). They reach $0.753$--$0.835$, against $0.488$--$0.689$ for CEM on the same energies. For the forward model of Dynamics (Standard), the per-label readout lifts the score from $0.605$ (hard CEM) to $0.790$, reproducing Table~\ref{tab:cnn-vit-planning}; the CEM soft score differs from that table by $0.003$ because of different random draws. The readout alone does not reach the inverse model, however. On this forward model it stops at $0.790$, and its nothing-changed control ($0.782$) shows that almost all of the gain over carry-forward comes from $\vh_t$.

\paragraph{The full posterior needs an energy trained on the actions it weighs.} For every MSE energy, the posterior scores below the per-label readout. It reaches $0.763$ against $0.790$ for the forward model of Dynamics (Standard), and $0.753$--$0.784$ against $0.768$--$0.806$ for the MSE heads, and its nothing-changed controls sit at carry-forward ($0.743$--$0.761$). An MSE energy is fit only at the true action. Some of the multi-flip actions, which hold $36\%$ of the prior mass, therefore receive a low energy whatever the outcome, the weakness the search exploits in Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}. In the posterior they absorb mass and spread it over labels that did not change. The per-label readout never weighs these actions and is unaffected. Its validation temperature lies at large values ($0.3$--$1$), where $E$ mostly breaks ties within each label's present and absent groups (Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit}).

With prior decoys, the energy is trained on the same actions the posterior weighs. The posterior then matches or exceeds the per-label readout on all three feature sets, with $0.824$, $0.835$ and $0.812$ against $0.821$, $0.834$ and $0.808$. Its validation temperature, $\tau=0.03$ for all three, is close to the $\kappa$ that the InfoNCE term had learned by the selected epochs (about $0.025$--$0.03$), as the optimum above predicts. Probe decoys lack this property, because their multi-flip candidates are variants of the true action rather than draws from the prior, and with them the posterior falls to $0.798$, against $0.831$ per label.

\paragraph{Together, the two changes match the inverse model.} With prior decoys and either marginal readout, the forward model scores $0.821$--$0.824$ on the Dynamics (Standard) features and $0.834$--$0.835$ on the Inverse Dynamics features. The Inverse Dynamics model scores $0.827$, and the inverse head refit on its frozen features $0.828$. These differences are at most $0.008$, well inside the intervals, so the forward model matches the inverse model but does not exceed it. Neither change suffices alone. The marginal readouts of MSE energies reach at most $0.806$, and $0.790$ on the forward model of Dynamics (Standard); CEM on the prior-decoy energies reaches at most $0.689$.

\paragraph{The matched score is read mostly from $\vh_t$.} The nothing-changed controls separate the forward and inverse models. The prior-decoy energies keep $0.816$ and $0.808$ with the posterior readout when the outcome is replaced by $\vh_t$, whereas the inverse head falls to $0.752$, close to carry-forward. The outcome ECG therefore adds $0.008$ and $0.027$ to the forward model's score, against $0.076$ to the inverse head's. On random features the gain is entirely from $\vh_t$ ($0.812$ against $0.811$). Because the prior decoys are drawn without looking at $\vh_t$, the cheapest way to tell the true transition from them is its plausibility for this patient. The energy therefore learns which transitions the current ECG makes likely, and uses the outcome only weakly. The contrastive energies are also worse next-state predictors than the MSE heads. On validation, their prediction error relative to doing nothing is about $0.79$, against $0.68$, on Inverse Dynamics features, and about $1.3$, against $0.95$, on Dynamics (Standard) features, where it is worse than doing nothing. Among the forward energies, the most outcome evidence remains in the MSE head on Inverse Dynamics features ($0.806$ against $0.751$ per label, a gain of $0.055$), at a lower absolute score. A planner at $0.82$--$0.84$ is thus mostly a learned prior over transitions given the current ECG, not a forward simulation of the observed change.

\paragraph{Summary and limitations.} The forward model can be made to classify as well as the inverse model, but only by taking both directions left open in Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}. The readout must compute marginals instead of searching for one action. For the full posterior, the energy must also be trained contrastively against decoys drawn from the readout's own prior. Even then, most of what the forward model adds over carry-forward is read from $\vh_t$, whereas the inverse model reads the change.

These results come from heads trained on frozen features in a second stage, not from the end-to-end pretraining objective, with one seed per head and point estimates. The base-rate prior also factorizes over labels. It puts $28.5\%$ of its mass on no change, while $48.8\%$ of the test pairs have none. Two variants remain untested. The first would draw the decoys, and the readout's prior, from a learned $p(\va\mid\vh_t,\vy_t)$. The energy would then have only the outcome to discriminate with, and the readout would separate dynamics evidence from the prior by construction. The second would use CEM's adapted population as the proposal, with importance weights for the prior, to concentrate the samples where the posterior has mass.


\subsection{Diagnosis Transitions as Weak, Confounded and Partially Grounded Labels}
\label{sec:gonogo}

The preceding sections converge on one observation. The diagnosis transition $\va_t$ explains almost none of the change between a patient's ECGs, the forward model fits the action effects of its training transitions without transferring them, and pretraining without actions performs as well as pretraining with them (Appendices~\ref{sec:cnn-vit} and~\ref{sec:no-actions}). This section asks why, and whether the answer supports a different use of the labels. It reports the first part of a go/no-go study of one hypothesis: that ICD transitions are not actions but weak, confounded and partially grounded annotations of the transition. All results in this section were computed on a single frozen encoder, together with signal features, and they are provisional until that encoder is checked against an alternative (Appendix~\ref{sec:gonogo-decision}).

\subsubsection{Hypothesis and decision rules}
\label{sec:gonogo-hypothesis}

\paragraph{Labels as annotations.} We model the label change as an instance-dependent noisy annotation of the transition, $\va_t=g(\text{true transition},\text{patient state},\text{context},\text{noise})$, in which some coordinates have no effect on the signal at all. Three properties follow. The labels are \emph{weak}, because they are recorded after the fact, with omissions, delays and errors. They are \emph{partially grounded}, because many codes describe conditions without an electrophysiological signature. And they can be \emph{confounded}, because codes are assigned within a clinical context, such as an admission or an acute illness, that also changes the ECG. The context can act through two routes. Through the \emph{event route}, it changes which conditions occur; through the \emph{recording route}, it changes which conditions are written down, for example because an admission documents more comorbidities than a visit to the emergency department. Under either route, a code without any route to the ECG still predicts the transition, since it marks a context that does. Any criterion that keeps a code because it improves prediction, including the magnitude of a learned action effect, a sparsity pattern, or a conditional mutual information, then keeps confounded codes for the wrong reason.

\paragraph{Two conditions.} The study requires two conditions before a method built on this hypothesis is developed. Condition C1, the subject of this section, is that confounding of the transition is measurably present in MIMIC-IV-ECG. Condition C2, not yet tested, is that existing world-model baselines declare ungrounded but confounded codes grounded when the confounder is a real one from these data, and increasingly so as confounding grows in a controlled simulation. If either condition fails, the confounding axis is dropped and the project is re-scoped.

\paragraph{Decision rule for C1.} For each code $c$ and each target $\Delta\in\{\Delta\vm,\Delta\vb,\Delta\vh\}$ (defined below), we ask whether the onset $a_{t,c}$ carries information about the change beyond the current state, under three nested conditioning sets: elapsed time only, the observed encounter covariates $\vz_t$, and $\vz_t$ together with the labels of a set of \emph{anchor} codes chosen in advance for having no route to the ECG. Codes fall into the reading of an interpretation matrix. A gain on the signal targets and on $\Delta\vh$ that does not change with conditioning is read as \emph{grounded}. A gain on $\Delta\vh$ alone that disappears under $\vz_t$ is \emph{confounded by observed context}, and one that persists under $\vz_t$ but disappears with the anchors is \emph{confounded by context that the anchors proxy}, the case a deconfounding method is built for. A gain on $\Delta\vh$ that persists under both is either an unobserved confounder or a real effect outside the measured features. For codes without a plausible route to the ECG, it is read as confounding. A gain on the signal targets without one on $\Delta\vh$ means the encoder lost the information. C1 is GO if at least one negative-control or ambiguous code shows a significant gain on $\Delta\vh$ after Benjamini--Hochberg correction, and that gain shrinks substantially under $\vz_t$ or under $\vz_t$ and the anchors. The rule fixes in advance how the conditioning set that removes the gain is read. Removal by $\vz_t$ is observed confounding: conditioning on $\vz_t$ fixes it, and the case for a method then rests on such variables often being unrecorded. Removal only by the anchors is the case the method is built for. The strongest GO combines a residual after $\vz_t$ that the anchors remove with direct evidence for the recording route. C1 is NO-GO if no code outside the positive group beats its placebo on $\Delta\vh$, and inconclusive if the positive controls fail, in which case the representations are fixed and the analysis rerun.

\subsubsection{Data, representations and estimator}
\label{sec:gonogo-data}

\paragraph{Linkage and pairs.} MIMIC-IV-ECG-Ext-ICD provides, for every ECG, the emergency stay, the admission that stay led to, the admission the ECG was taken in, age, sex, fold and the stay counter used in Appendix~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix}. Joined on the study identifier, it reproduces our ECG table exactly, including subjects, folds, stay counters and acquisition times to the second, and its \texttt{all\_diag\_all} column truncated to three characters reproduces the 76 labels on every ECG. It is therefore the full-chapter version of our labels, which we use for non-cardiac codes below. A stay is the admission when the ECG was taken in hospital, and the emergency stay otherwise. An emergency-only stay is labelled with its emergency discharge codes, and an admission with its hospital discharge codes.

This linkage shows that the cross-stay pairs of Appendix~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix} contain two kinds of pair. In 44{,}684 of the 166{,}229 cross-stay pairs ($26.9\%$), the two stays belong to one episode: an emergency visit and the admission it led to. Both ECGs of such a pair carry the discharge codes of the same admission, so the pair is stable by construction ($100.00\%$ of them are). These pairs make up $99\%$ of the cross-stay pairs recorded less than 12 hours apart and $65\%$ of those 1 to 7 days apart. They are why $48.3\%$ of the cross-stay pairs in Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats} are stable. We exclude them and use them only as a noise floor. Of the remaining 121{,}545 pairs between episodes, $29.3\%$ are stable, 85{,}906 change at least one code and 30{,}863 change exactly one; 118{,}670 have usable features at both ECGs. We never pair consecutive ECGs regardless of stay. An ECG recorded outside any stay has missing codes, stored as zeros, and such pairs would add 119{,}220 artificial transitions whose presence is decided by the care setting.

The \emph{clean} subset, which is the primary analysis, keeps the stable pairs and the single-code transitions, so that no other cardiac code changes together with the code under test. For a non-cardiac code, its clean analogue keeps the pairs on which no cardiac code changes. The \emph{adjusted} subset keeps every pair at risk for an onset and adds the onset and resolution indicators of all other cardiac codes as covariates. It serves as a sensitivity analysis. A code enters the study if it has at least 250 clean onsets over the 20 patient-stratified folds; 13 of the 76 cardiac codes pass.

\paragraph{Representations and controls.} The study uses three representations, none trained with labels or actions. The representation $\vh$ is the 256-dimensional embedding of the ViT pretrained without actions (Appendix~\ref{sec:no-actions}), read at its checkpoint with the lowest validation loss (epoch 339), which uses no labels. The features $\vb$ are the 39 median-beat features of the signal: heart rate, RR irregularity, a QRS-width proxy, and per-lead QRS amplitude, ST level and T amplitude. The features $\vm$ are 12 measurements of the acquisition cart from \texttt{machine\_measurements}: heart rate, PR, QRS and QT intervals, Bazett-corrected QTc, the three electrical axes as cosine and sine, and an indicator of a missing P wave. Missing values in that file are sentinels, which we treat as missing, with physiologically impossible values discarded. Its acquisition date disagrees with ours for $2.2\%$ of ECGs, by days to decades, so we join on the study identifier only; the measurements nevertheless belong to the right waveforms, since its heart rate correlates with ours at $r=0.98$. The targets are the changes $\Delta\vh$, $\Delta\vb$ and $\Delta\vm$ between the two ECGs of a pair, each dimension standardized. The state is $\vs_t=[\vb_t,\vh_t,\vm_t]$, so that every target's current value, including the heart rate at $t$, is controlled for. The observed encounter covariates $\vz_t$ are the logarithm of the elapsed time, the care setting at $t$ and at $t+1$ (emergency only, emergency then admitted, or inpatient), the coding system in force at each stay, and age and sex. The coding system matters because the labels convert ICD-9 codes to ICD-10, and $9.5\%$ of the pairs straddle the 2015 switch. It is read from the admission's diagnosis records, and for emergency-only stays it is inferred from the patient's admissions around the switch, which leaves $7.2\%$ of pairs with an unknown system. Variables that can be consequences of the transition, such as admission to intensive care, triage acuity or length of stay, are kept out of $\vz_t$.

\paragraph{Codes.} The positive controls are the five conduction and rhythm codes with established ECG signatures: I44 (atrioventricular and left bundle branch block), I45 (other conduction disorders), I47 (paroxysmal tachycardia), I48 (atrial fibrillation and flutter) and I49 (other arrhythmias). Between episodes they have only 129 to 777 clean onsets, so only I47 and I48 reach the threshold of 250. The negative controls and anchors come from the full-chapter labels, and their roles were fixed from label statistics alone, before any gain was computed. Of the 151 non-cardiac codes with at least 250 clean onsets, 117 were excluded, each with a recorded reason. These reasons were electrolytes, fluids or nutrition; drugs with ECG effects; heart rate through fever, anaemia, bleeding or hypoxia; endocrine or cardiovascular risk factors; pulmonary disease; cardiac symptoms, devices or history; the brain; the liver; and nonspecific or external-cause codes. External-cause chapters are excluded in particular because the labels keep ICD-9 history codes whose strings are also valid ICD-10 codes. One further code, do-not-resuscitate status, failed a separation screen. Its onsets raised the odds of a grounded cardiac onset on the same pair, beyond setting, age and sex, by a factor of $1.60$, above the screen's threshold of $1.5$; the median over the retained pool is $1.02$. The remaining 33 codes were split at random, with a fixed seed and within two categories, into three disjoint roles (Table~\ref{tab:gonogo-roles}). The categories separate codes that are also recorded at emergency-only stays from codes recorded essentially only during admissions. The ambiguous codes are the nine non-positive cardiac codes that reach the event threshold, among them essential hypertension (I10), chronic ischaemic heart disease (I25) and heart failure (I50). Two venous codes, I82 and I87, form a secondary set of weak negative controls.

\paragraph{Estimator.} Two separately trained predictors of the change, one with the onset and one without, differ by about $10^{-4}$ in held-out loss from seed to seed on $\Delta\vb$, even with shared initialization and batches. This exceeds the gains of the smaller positive controls. The primary statistic is therefore a cross-fitted R-learner gain. Let $\hat f$ be a base predictor of the standardized change from $\vs_t$ and the conditioning set: an MLP with two hidden layers, averaged over three seeds, whose fold-$k$ model is fitted on 18 other folds and stopped early on the next one. Let $\hat\pi$ be a cross-fitted logistic propensity for the onset given the same inputs. For each target dimension $j$, the effect $\hat\theta_j^{(-k)}=\sum(a-\hat\pi)(\Delta_j-\hat f_j)\big/\sum(a-\hat\pi)^2$ is fitted on the folds other than $k$, and the gain is the held-out reduction in squared error obtained by adding it,
$$ G_c=\frac{1}{NJ}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\Big[\big(\Delta_{ij}-\hat f_j(i)\big)^2-\big(\Delta_{ij}-\hat f_j(i)-(a_i-\hat\pi_i)\,\hat\theta_j^{(-k(i))}\big)^2\Big]. $$
The correction is close to zero on pairs without an onset, whose propensity is small, much like a per-code channel that is silent when its label does not change. The gain is also doubly robust, since under no association its bias requires both $\hat f$ and $\hat\pi$ to be wrong. Significance comes from a conditional randomization test. We draw 1{,}000 placebo onsets from the propensity at the real prevalence, recompute the gain for each, and take the rank of the real one. The test is exact when the propensity is correct. A permutation test that shuffles onsets within strata of elapsed time, care setting at $t+1$ and age agrees with it on $\Delta\vh$ once $\vz_t$ is conditioned on. With elapsed time as the only control, it is more conservative on $\Delta\vb$ and $\Delta\vm$, because its strata already hold part of $\vz_t$ fixed. The placebo gain has a negative mean, the held-out cost of fitting $\hat\theta$ for a label with no signal, so we report the \emph{excess gain} $\bar G_c=G_c-\mathbb{E}_{\text{placebo}}[G_c]$, in units of $10^{-5}$ of the per-dimension change variance. Zero means as uninformative as a placebo onset. Intervals are patient-level bootstrap intervals (1{,}000 resamples). Ratios between conditioning sets use paired resamples, since the analysis set of a code does not depend on the conditioning. Multiplicity is controlled with Benjamini--Hochberg at $q=0.05$, separately for the positive controls and for the remaining codes, per target and conditioning set. The two-model gain is kept as a secondary statistic. A gain can be significant while its bootstrap interval includes zero. The randomization test conditions on the observed changes and asks whether the onset is associated with them, whereas the bootstrap also carries the sampling variability of the effect's size.

\subsubsection{The recording route}
\label{sec:gonogo-recording}

Because emergency-only stays are labelled with emergency discharge codes and admissions with hospital discharge codes, the care setting decides which document a label comes from. We measured this route with labels alone, by logistic regression over the pairs between episodes. The outcome was the disappearance of a code present at $t$, or the appearance of an absent one, as a function of the setting at $t+1$, adjusted for the setting at $t$, elapsed time, age, sex and a change of coding system. Intervals are clustered by patient. Chronic cardiac codes usually disappear when the next stay is emergency-only (Table~\ref{tab:gonogo-recording}). Heart failure disappears from $95\%$ of such pairs against $18\%$ when the next stay is an admission, an odds ratio of 95. The rates for chronic ischaemic disease are $90\%$ against $16\%$, and for atrial fibrillation $73\%$ against $13\%$. Much of the label change between stays is therefore a property of the document, not of the patient. The non-cardiac codes show the same pattern more starkly. Those recorded essentially only during admissions disappear at every emergency-only stay (median $100\%$, against $66\%$ at an admission). Acute complaints go the other way, with onsets of abdominal pain and headache 3.7 and 2.6 times as likely into an emergency-only stay. The change of coding system also matters: crossing the 2015 switch raises onsets of atrial fibrillation (odds ratio $1.43$) and heart failure ($1.28$), and it changes onset rates for 20 of the 37 codes examined. The recording route is thus present, and for chronic codes it is close to deterministic. Both of its observed drivers, the setting and the coding system, are part of $\vz_t$.

\subsubsection{Is the transition confounded?}
\label{sec:gonogo-c1}

\paragraph{The positive controls pass.} Every positive control shows a significant gain on $\Delta\vm$ or $\Delta\vb$ with the full $\vz_t$ (Table~\ref{tab:gonogo-positive}), which the decision rule requires before anything else is read. The effects are the expected ones. Onsets of I44 and I45 widen the machine QRS duration by $0.91$ and $0.90$ standard deviations of the change, about 9~ms. Onsets of I47 raise the heart rate by $0.57$ standard deviations, about 11~bpm. Onsets of I48 remove the P wave ($+1.39$) and raise the heart rate by 11 to 12~bpm. The irregular rhythm of I49 is thus measured by the median-beat features and lost by the encoder. Conditioning on $\vz_t$ changes the positive controls' gains by $10\%$ or less, as expected of grounded codes.

\subsubsection{Can the label vector stand in for the context?}
\label{sec:gonogo-labels}

The eleven anchors add nothing beyond $\vz_t$. This leaves two questions. The first is whether a larger set of codes could remove what $\vz_t$ leaves. The second is whether labels could replace $\vz_t$ when it is not recorded, which is the situation a deconfounding method would face.

\paragraph{Upper bound for label-based deconfounding.} We took every non-cardiac code with at least 200 transitions in the training folds, 505 in all, and split them by a documented rule into two sets. Set R holds the 297 codes with a plausible route to the ECG, each with a reason, such as electrolyte disorders, thyroid disease, kidney failure, sepsis, chronic lung disease, anaemia, drugs and cardiac history. Set A holds the remaining 208, which have no plausible route. A includes all 33 codes of Table~\ref{tab:gonogo-roles}. We then added the onset and resolution indicators of R, of A, or of both to $\vz_t$, always excluding the code under test, and measured the fraction of the gain left after $\vz_t$ that each set removes (Table~\ref{tab:gonogo-labelsets}). Set A removes nothing, with fractions between $-0.07$ and $0.18$, all of whose intervals include zero. Set R removes part of the residual for some codes: $0.35$ $[0.07, 1.28]$ for I85 on $\Delta\vh$ and $0.42$ $[0.23, 0.91]$ for I50 on $\Delta\vb$. Under R, constipation also loses significance (from $1.9$ to $1.3$), although that fraction's interval is wide. But R also removes $18$ to $22\%$ of the positive controls' effects, which are real. Conditioning on codes that have their own route to the ECG thus absorbs part of genuine effects, through mediators or co-consequences such as anticoagulation after atrial fibrillation. From these data, that over-adjustment cannot be told apart from confounding by those conditions.

\paragraph{Labels as a substitute for missing metadata.} When the observed covariates are withheld instead of present, the picture changes (Table~\ref{tab:gonogo-substitute}). For each code whose gain $\vz_t$ reduces, we withheld the setting at $t+1$, age and sex, or all of $\vz_t$. We then measured which fraction of the lost removal is recovered by adding either the eleven anchors or the full no-route set A. For the codes driven by the setting, A recovers a substantial share of what the setting removes. The primary reading withholds only the setting at $t+1$, and there A recovers $0.42$ to $1.30$ of the removal, with intervals excluding zero for two of the five codes, hypotension and oesophageal disease. When all of $\vz_t$ is withheld, it recovers $0.68$ to $1.00$, with intervals excluding zero for all five, but that reading also withholds age and sex, which no label set recovers. The eleven anchors recover a third to two thirds of $\vz_t$'s removal for three codes on $\Delta\vb$, and nothing on $\Delta\vh$. Neither set recovers what age and sex remove. Those variables are always recorded, however, whereas the setting and the provenance of a label are what a dataset may lack. The label vector therefore carries the provenance of the labels, and only a broad set of codes recovers it, not a short list. A provenance check is needed to tell whether it carries more than provenance (Appendix~\ref{sec:gonogo-decision}).

\paragraph{The residual of constipation.} Only one residual after $\vz_t$ is robust: that of constipation, which a real ECG effect cannot plausibly explain. It survives both subsets and every label set. We tested two further explanations (Table~\ref{tab:gonogo-residual}). The first is a co-occurring physiological cause. Within 24 hours of each ECG we measured potassium, magnesium and thyroid-stimulating hormone, and recorded any administration of opioids or laxatives, from the inpatient administration record, prescriptions and emergency dispensing. Potassium was available for $97\%$ of ECGs, magnesium for $73\%$, TSH for $12\%$, and $43\%$ and $53\%$ of ECGs were exposed to opioids and laxatives. These covariates predict constipation strongly, raising its propensity's AUROC from $0.69$ to $0.79$. Yet they remove only $14\%$ of its residual, with an interval including zero, and they leave both K59 and K80 significant. They also remove $12\%$ $[0, 31\%]$ of the effect of atrial fibrillation, consistent with their being partly post-event variables. The second explanation is a misspecified propensity. Replacing the logistic propensity with an MLP or with gradient-boosted trees leaves the residual unchanged, at $1.8$ to $2.0$ for K59 and $1.8$ to $1.9$ for K80. Neither flexible model predicts the onsets as well as the logistic one, so a better propensity was not available to test. Because the estimator is doubly robust, a propensity artefact would also have required a wrong base predictor. The residual therefore remains unexplained.

\subsubsection{What the representation retains}
\label{sec:gonogo-representation}

The embedding is the weakest of the three representations for this purpose. We estimated each positive control's effect on $\Delta\vh$ as the vector of cross-fitted effects $\hat\theta$ over the 256 dimensions. The plug-in squared norm of such a vector is biased upward by its estimation noise, so we instead took the inner product of two estimates from disjoint halves of the folds, which share no patients. Only atrial fibrillation's effect vector has a squared norm whose interval excludes zero: $0.69$ $[0.14, 1.29]$ in standardized units. The other controls' intervals include zero (I44 $1.90$ $[-0.56, 4.80]$, I45 $2.41$ $[-0.10, 5.48]$, I47 $0.42$ $[-0.68, 1.56]$, I49 $0.66$ $[-1.73, 2.99]$), and the plug-in norms are 1.6 to 4.8 times larger than these estimates. Atrial fibrillation's gain is about 25 times larger per dimension on $\Delta\vb$ than on $\Delta\vh$ ($143$ against $5.7$). A cross-fitted decomposition of the change between episodes points the same way. The 152 transition indicators of the cardiac codes explain $R^2=0.0002$ of $\Delta\vh$ beyond elapsed time, against $0.0041$ of $\Delta\vb$ and $0.0064$ of $\Delta\vm$. The current state explains $0.13$, $0.25$ and $0.21$, through regression toward the mean. Patient identity accounts for $0.74$ of the variance of $\vh$ itself, but for none of the change. The noise floor is high in every representation. Pairs on which no cardiac code changes move $0.88$ times as much in $\vh$ as the average pair between episodes (changed pairs $1.05$), and same-episode pairs $0.72$ times as much. In $\vb$ the corresponding values are $0.72$, $1.12$ and $0.88$, and within-stay pairs move $1.00$ times as much. These findings are consistent with an encoder that suppresses within-patient change. This is the expected effect of its pretraining objective, which predicts a patient's next recording from the current one and so rewards invariance across that patient's recordings.

\subsubsection{Outcome, limitations and next steps}
\label{sec:gonogo-decision}

\paragraph{Outcome of C1.} By the decision rule fixed in advance, C1 is GO for confounding by observed context. Codes without a plausible route to the ECG, and ambiguous codes, carry significant information about the transition that the observed covariates partly remove, while the gains of the positive controls do not move. The drivers are age and sex and the care setting at $t+1$, and the recording route behind the second of these is measured directly and is close to deterministic for chronic codes. The strongest form of GO is not reached. No set of codes removes the residual left after $\vz_t$: not the eleven anchors, not the 208 no-route codes, and not laboratory or medication covariates. The 297 route codes weaken it but also absorb part of real effects. The one robust residual, that of constipation, is unexplained. Under the rule's first branch, conditioning on $\vz_t$ fixes the confounding it removes, and the case for a method rests on such variables being unrecorded.

The label-substitution analysis tests that case. When the care setting is withheld, a broad rule-based set of no-route codes recovers a substantial share of what it removes, with point estimates of $0.42$ to $1.30$ and intervals excluding zero for two of five codes, whereas the curated list of eleven anchors does not. The value of label-based deconfounding thus lies where encounter metadata are missing, and it requires many weak proxies rather than a few strong ones. For a deconfounding method, the anchor set should be the full set of codes classified as having no route under a documented rule. Its estimate of the context should be expected to recover provenance and care setting, not the residual. Unexplained residuals should be bounded by a margin estimated from held-out no-route codes rather than assumed away.

\paragraph{Limitations.} All results rest on one frozen encoder, whose objective rewards invariance across a patient's recordings, and the positive-control effects in its embedding are weak. Conclusions about $\Delta\vh$ may change with a representation that keeps more of the within-patient change. The clean subset gives the positive controls only 129 to 777 onsets, so several of their effects are estimated with wide intervals. The randomization test is exact only for a correct propensity, and the logistic model, although the best of the three tried, is not known to be correct. The secondary permutation test used 100 permutations, too few for its results to survive correction. The laboratory and medication covariates may be consequences of the transition, so their small effect on the residual is a bound rather than a test. Each analysis used three seeds of one predictor family. Condition C2 has not been tested.

\paragraph{Next steps.} Three checks precede the test of C2. The first compares the debiased positive-control effect norms, in units of each representation's noise floor, across four candidates: the current embedding, an encoder trained on single recordings without same-patient positives, $\vb$, and the concatenation of $\vh$ and $\vb$. It then repeats the label-set analyses on the best of them. The second asks how well the no-route set alone predicts the care setting at $t+1$, and whether its recovery survives within pairs whose setting does not change. The answer separates provenance from any further context the labels carry. The third estimates the residual-bias margin by rotation over held-out no-route codes, always excluding the code under test, and asks which positive controls clear it. The test of C2 then injects atrial fibrillation's effect vector, the only one estimated reliably, into real pairs, uses the care setting at $t+1$ as the real confounder, and gives every method the real codes, including the no-route set.
