\subsection{Pairing system}
\label{sec:pairing-appendix}

Longitudinal pairs $(\mX_t, \mX_{t+1})$ for pretraining are constructed directly from MIMIC-IV-ECG's hospital-stay structure, using \texttt{ecg\_no\_within\_stay} (enws) as the per-admission ECG counter and \texttt{subject\_id} to group all ECGs belonging to the same patient, time-ordered by \texttt{ecg\_time}. Two pair types are distinguished:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Within-stay}: consecutive ECGs recorded during the same hospital stay, i.e.\ enws increasing between two chronologically adjacent records for a patient. Since ICD-10 diagnosis codes in this dataset are assigned at the hospitalization level (one code set per admission), $\vy_t = \vy_{t+1}$ for essentially every within-stay pair; the transition $\va_t = \text{clip}(\vy_{t+1}-\vy_t,-1,1)$ is almost always the zero vector, so this pair type contributes encoder training signal from waveform variability alone, not from a genuine diagnostic transition.
\item \textbf{Cross-stay}: the first ECG of stay $N$ paired with the first ECG of stay $N+1$ for the same patient (enws $=0$ on both sides), i.e.\ pairing across separate admissions rather than within one. Because separate hospitalizations frequently carry different discharge diagnoses, this is where the pathology-transition signal $\va_t \ne \vec{0}$ actually concentrates.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats} reports, on the training split (folds 0--17, 721{,}002 exams), the pair count, the stable/active split (stable: $\va_t=\vec{0}$; active: $\lVert\va_t\rVert_0>0$), and the mean number of flipped ICD-10 columns among active pairs, for each pair type. As expected from the per-admission label assignment, within-stay pairs are stable in 160{,}371/160{,}400 ($99.98\%$) of cases; cross-stay pairs are close to evenly split (72{,}386 stable / 77{,}537 active), confirming that admission-to-admission transitions are the primary source of diagnostic-change supervision for the dynamics objective.

\paragraph{Multistep cross-stay pairing.} The cross-stay pairing above only links stay $N$ to stay $N+1$ (hop distance $k=1$). We additionally explore pairing stay $N$ to stay $N+k$ for growing $k$, pooling all hops up to $k$ into a single, larger cross-stay pair set (all such pairs stay within one patient, so pooling introduces no fold leakage). For a patient with $L$ recorded stays, hop $k$ contributes $\max(0, L-k)$ pairs; across the 111{,}573 training patients with at least one stay (mean 2.34, median 1, max 125 stays per patient), $k=1$ alone reproduces the 149{,}923-pair count of Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats} exactly, which we use as a consistency check between the two pair-construction scripts.

\subsection{Pairing Scheme Ablation: Pretraining Dynamics}
\label{sec:pairing-ablation}

We compare three pretraining configurations of the forward Dynamics model (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}), differing only in which pairs the SSL objective is trained on: (i) the pooled baseline, using both within-stay and cross-stay pairs from the traditional pair index ($k=1$ only); (ii) cross-stay-only, restricting the same traditional pair index to cross-stay pairs; and (iii) multistep cross-stay, pooling cross-stay pairs at every hop $k=1,\dots,12$ (Section~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix}). All three share the same encoder architecture, batch size, and optimizer configuration; only \texttt{pairs\_path} and \texttt{pair\_types} differ.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{papel/fig/pairing_ablation_losses.png}
\caption{Validation loss curves for the three pairing configurations of Section~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation}: SIGReg regularization loss (left), dynamics prediction loss (middle), and total loss (right), against training step.}
\label{fig:pairing-ablation-losses}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:pairing-ablation-losses} shows two effects not anticipated from encoder architecture alone. First, cross-stay-only pretraining reaches a visibly higher validation \texttt{pred\_loss} than the pooled baseline, consistent with training on substantially fewer pairs under the same epoch budget: pooling within-stay and cross-stay pairs yields 310{,}323 training pairs (Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats}), versus 149{,}923 for cross-stay alone, so the restricted run sees under half the unique pairs per epoch and overfits sooner despite within-stay pairs being label-stable ($99.98\%$) and thus contributing mostly waveform-level, rather than transition-level, training signal. Second, the multistep run's \texttt{reg\_loss} sits roughly $5\times$ higher than the baseline's. The SIGReg statistic (Appendix~\ref{sec:sigreg} % TODO: fix ref if the SIGReg formula is documented under a different label
) is a sliced Epps--Pulley goodness-of-fit statistic that is $O(1)$ under a well-matched (near-Gaussian) embedding distribution but scales linearly with the effective batch size used in its estimator under any sustained deviation from it; since $\lambda$ was calibrated against the baseline configuration's regularizer scale, a shift in that scale — whether from genuinely harder-to-regularize embeddings under the larger, more heterogeneous multistep pair pool, or from an unpinned world-size/batch-scale confound across runs — changes the effective SSL/regularization balance without $\lambda$ itself having been retuned. %TODO: confirm via train/loss_ratio logs and matched world_size across runs before drawing a final conclusion here; update this paragraph once confirmed.

\subsection{Embedding-Space Diagnostic}
\label{sec:embedding-diagnostic}

To probe what each of the three pretrained Dynamics checkpoints from Section~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation} has actually learned beyond the pretraining loss curves, we run a post-hoc diagnostic on a random batch of validation pairs. For each pair, we compute the actual current- and next-state embeddings $\vh_t=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_t)$, $\vh_{t+1}=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_{t+1})$, and three candidate predictions of $\vh_{t+1}$ from $\vh_t$: $\hat{\vh}_{t+1}=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ using the pair's true transition; a zero-action baseline $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\vec{0}))$; and a noise-action baseline $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\tilde{\va}_t))$ using a synthetic ternary vector $\tilde{\va}_t$ magnitude-matched to the pair's own true action — $\lVert\tilde{\va}_t\rVert_0=\lVert\va_t\rVert_0$, with flips placed on uniformly random label positions and random $\pm1$ signs — i.e.\ ``right sparsity, wrong labels,'' isolating whether $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ uses \emph{which} labels changed rather than merely how many. We score all three against $\vh_{t+1}$ by MSE and by cosine similarity, both broken down by transition magnitude $\lVert\va_t\rVert_0$ (the number of ICD-10 columns that flip) into all pairs, active pairs ($\lVert\va_t\rVert_0>0$), and stable pairs ($\va_t=\vec{0}$), to test whether each checkpoint's predictions specifically track the size of the diagnostic change rather than being uniformly accurate or uniformly insensitive to $\va_t$.

\subsection{Displacement-Conditioned Dynamics Variant}
\label{sec:displacement-diagnostic}

The Inverse Dynamics model (Section~\ref{sec:inverse}) is parameterized in terms of the latent displacement $\vd_t = \vh_{t+1}-\vh_t$ rather than the absolute next state. We implement the analogous reparameterization for the forward Dynamics model: $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ is trained to output a predicted displacement $\hat{\vd}_t=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ rather than a predicted next state, and the prediction term of the loss is computed directly against the true displacement,
\[
\mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda)\,\text{MSE}\big(\vh_{t+1}-\vh_t,\ \text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))\big) + \lambda\,\big(\text{SIGReg}(H_t)+\text{SIGReg}(H_{t+1})\big),
\]
with the architecture of $\text{Dyn}_\phi$, the SIGReg regularization, and all other hyperparameters (batch size, optimizer, $\lambda$, learning rate) unchanged from the standard next-state formulation of Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}, so that the two variants differ only in what the predictor's output is trained to represent.

To probe this variant with the same methodology as Section~\ref{sec:embedding-diagnostic}, we reuse its do-nothing / true-action / zero-action / noise-action comparison, reconstructing a next-state prediction for each condition as $\vh_t+\hat{\vd}_t$ so that all four columns of Table~\ref{tab:embedding-mse-displacement} remain directly comparable, in units of MSE to $\vh_{t+1}$, to the corresponding columns of Table~\ref{tab:embedding-mse}.

\subsection{CEM Planning with the Forward Dynamics Model}
\label{sec:cem-planning}

This section evaluates the Planning procedure of Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}, in which diagnosis is recovered by searching for the transition $\va^\star$ that the forward model $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ judges to best explain an observed pair of embeddings. We report it in full because the outcome is negative, and because the way it fails is informative about what the forward model has, and has not, learned.

\paragraph{Setup.} The search follows Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}: two Bernoulli heads, onset and resolution, masked by the known previous label $\vy_{t-1}$ so that only admissible transitions are proposed. Both are initialized at Laplace-smoothed per-class base rates estimated on the 149{,}923 training cross-stay pairs. The onset rate has mean $0.0104$ and median $0.0028$; the resolution rate has mean $0.476$ and median $0.476$. Each CEM run uses a population of $N=512$ candidates, $K=64$ elites and $16$ iterations. The elite refit keeps weight $0.1$ on the previous distribution. Every Bernoulli parameter is clamped to $[0.005, 0.995]$ so that rare onsets remain explorable. The do-nothing action seeds the incumbent, so $E(\va^\star)\le E(\vec 0)$ by construction. We verified the search on a synthetic oracle energy $E(\va)=\lVert\va-\va_{\text{gt}}\rVert^2$: it recovers the true action exactly for every pair, and all $2.1$M sampled candidates were admissible.

We evaluate all seven forward checkpoints: the three pairing configurations of Section~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation}, the displacement variant of Section~\ref{sec:displacement-diagnostic}, and, for the three next-state configurations, the twin checkpoint selected by the online linear-probe AUROC rather than by validation loss. Evaluation uses exactly the protocol of the inverse model and the carry-forward baseline: the 8{,}062 cross-stay test pairs ($k=1$, fold 19), macro AUROC over classes with both outcomes present, and 1{,}000 bootstrap resamples with a fixed seed. Onset and resolution AUROCs are computed only over the labels where each head is live ($\vy_{t-1,i}=0$ and $\vy_{t-1,i}=1$ respectively). Scoring them over all entries would credit the planner with the structural knowledge that a present label cannot have an onset.

\paragraph{Reference numbers.} Recomputed on the current version of the pair index, the carry-forward baseline $\hat{\vy}_t=\vy_{t-1}$ reaches $0.7515$ $[0.7361$--$0.7682]$ and the inverse dynamics model reaches $0.8286$ $[0.8124$--$0.8456]$ contextualized macro AUROC.
The inverse model's (unmasked) onset and resolution AUROCs are $0.6591$ and $0.6958$.

\paragraph{Minimizing reconstruction error alone.} Table~\ref{tab:cem-test} reports the planner with candidates ranked by $E(\va)$ alone. For every checkpoint, the contextualized AUROC falls far below carry-forward. The soft readout is at or near chance ($0.45$--$0.55$), and the hard readout reaches only $0.57$--$0.61$.

The diagnostics isolate the cause in the scoring function, not in the search:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The true action carries no signal.} The ground-truth transition beats the do-nothing action in only $47$--$54\%$ of active pairs, where chance is $50\%$. When it does, the mean reduction in $E$ is at most $0.005$ (Table~\ref{tab:cem-test}). This reproduces, on the full test set and for all seven checkpoints, the earlier finding on a single checkpoint that raw reconstruction error does not favour the true action on held-out data.
\item \textbf{The search exploits the model.} CEM lowers $E$ by $40$--$75\%$ relative to the do-nothing action. It does so with $6$--$12$ onsets per pair, against $0.67$ in the ground truth, and onset precision stays below $2\%$.
\item \textbf{Every active pair is a model failure.} In every active pair, the returned action scores at least as well as the true one, and it is never the true one. The search is not missing the true action; the model assigns lower error to transitions that never occur.
\end{itemize}
In other words, $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ is \emph{controllable} but not \emph{identifiable}: the $2^C$ admissible action codes, most far outside the training distribution of $\approx 2.5$ flips per active pair, span enough of the displacement space to fit a large part of $\vh_t-\vh_{t-1}$, while the one realistic code, the true transition, fits no better than doing nothing.

\paragraph{Prior-regularized objective.} Ranking candidates by $F(\va)=E(\va)/\tau+\sum_{i:\va_i\ne0}\log\frac{1-\pi_i}{\pi_i}$ instead (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}) makes every flip pay its prior log-odds: about $4.6$--$5.9$ nats for a typical onset, and close to zero, or negative, for a resolution. We swept $\tau\in\{3{\cdot}10^{-4},\dots,1\}$ on the validation split (8{,}244 cross-stay pairs, 100 bootstrap resamples) for all seven checkpoints (Table~\ref{tab:cem-tau}).

The prior removes the exploitation: onsets per pair fall from $8$--$12$ at $\tau\to0$ to $\approx0.3$ at $\tau=10^{-2}$, and to zero for $\tau\ge0.3$. The best soft AUROC improves accordingly, from $0.46$--$0.54$ to $0.57$--$0.59$, but it remains far below validation carry-forward ($0.758$). The hard readout peaks at $0.675$. At no temperature do the masked onset or resolution AUROCs leave the $0.50$--$0.53$ band, so the regularization suppresses spurious transitions without uncovering a real signal. This is consistent with the headroom test above, where the true transition does not lower $E$ in the first place.

\paragraph{Why the soft readout falls short of carry-forward even at large $\tau$.} As $\tau\to\infty$, $F$ reduces to the prior, whose posterior marginals are exactly the base rates. Reading those marginals through the contextualized combination gives $\vy_{t-1,i}(1-\pi^{\text{res}}_i)+(1-\vy_{t-1,i})\pi^{\text{ons}}_i$, a score that preserves the carry-forward ordering and so matches its AUROC.

CEM's final head parameters are not posterior marginals, however. The elite refit concentrates each Bernoulli on the mode, so every label whose resolution rate exceeds $0.5$ collapses to $p^{\text{res}}_i\approx1$. Its persistence score then falls to $\approx0.005$, tied with the onset scores of absent labels, and the carry-forward ordering is lost ($0.58$ instead of $0.758$). The hard readout suffers the same loss for the same reason: resolving every label whose prior favours resolution scores $0.669$.

A readout based on per-label conditional posteriors around the returned action, $p_i=\sigma\big(F(\va^\star_{i\to0})-F(\va^\star_{i\to\text{flip}})\big)$, would recover carry-forward in the prior limit and add the model's evidence at finite $\tau$. We leave it to future work. Given the headroom results above, we expect it to match, not exceed, carry-forward with the current checkpoints.

\paragraph{Summary.} With the present forward models, CEM planning does not recover diagnostic transitions. Minimizing reconstruction error alone is exploited through out-of-distribution action combinations, and adding the prior removes the exploitation without uncovering signal, because the ground-truth transition is no better than doing nothing under $\text{Dyn}_\phi$. The inverse dynamics model, trained to discriminate transitions directly, reaches $0.8286$ on the same pairs.

Making the forward model usable as a planner likely requires a training objective that explicitly penalizes wrong-action predictions, for example a contrastive term separating $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ from predictions under decoy actions. Besides the posterior-marginal readout, this is the main direction we leave for future work.

\subsection{Joint Forward and Inverse Pretraining}
\label{sec:joint-pretraining}

The two world-model components have complementary strengths. The forward Dynamics encoder transfers best under finetuning (Table~\ref{tab:classification}), but its forward model cannot be used for planning (Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}). The Inverse Dynamics model gives the best contextualized inference (Table~\ref{tab:contextualized}), with a weaker encoder. This section tests whether a single encoder trained under both objectives obtains both.

\paragraph{Setup.} The forward predictor $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ (with its projector $\text{Proj}_\omega$) and the inverse predictor $\text{Inv}_\psi$ share the encoder $\text{Enc}_\theta$ and nothing else; there is no coupling term between them. With $\vh_t=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_t)$ and $\vh_{t+1}=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_{t+1})$, the loss adds the prediction terms of Sections~\ref{sec:dynamics} and~\ref{sec:inverse} with equal weight,
\[
\mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda)\Big(\text{MSE}\big(\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t)),\,\vh_{t+1}\big) + \text{BCE}^{\text{ons}}_t + \text{BCE}^{\text{res}}_t\Big) + \lambda\big(\text{SIGReg}(\mH_t)+\text{SIGReg}(\mH_{t+1})\big),
\]
where $\text{BCE}^{\text{ons}}_t$ and $\text{BCE}^{\text{res}}_t$ are the onset and resolution terms of $\text{Inv}_\psi(\vh_{t+1}-\vh_t)$. Both terms are computed on every pair of the multistep cross-stay pool ($k\le12$, 605{,}533 training pairs; Appendix~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix}).

\subsection{Signal-Space Decoder}
\label{sec:decoder}

The diagnostics of Appendices~\ref{sec:embedding-diagnostic} and~\ref{sec:cem-planning} stay in latent space: both compare $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ with $\vh_{t+1}$ by squared error. Neither says whether $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ moves an embedding the way a diagnostic transition changes the ECG, for instance whether the onset of a conduction block reshapes the QRS complex.

We decode each estimate of $\vh_{t+1}$ and compare it with the real next ECG, and we decode the predictions under the true action and under control actions to see which of them reproduce the observed change. We write $\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\va)=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va))$, $\text{Dec}(\vh)$ for the decoded signal, and use the Standard forward checkpoint (within- and cross-stay pairs, selected by validation loss; Appendix~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation}).

\paragraph{Decoding targets.} The encoder pools over time, so $\vh$ does not record where the beats fall in the 10\,s window. On 8\,s crops of validation ECGs, shifting the crop by 20\,ms to 2\,s moves $\vh$ by only $0.3$--$0.8\%$ of the mean squared distance between two unrelated ECGs ($8$--$24\%$ for a randomly initialized encoder). A decoder of the raw $12\times5000$ strip would therefore learn an average over beat positions. We decode what $\vh$ can carry instead: the 12-lead median beat, from $0.4$\,s before to $0.6$\,s after the QRS complex ($12\times500$ samples at 500\,Hz, in the input's per-lead z-units), together with the heart rate and the RR irregularity (normalized RMSSD). Beats are extracted by detecting QRS complexes on the lead-summed squared slope of a 5--20\,Hz band-passed signal, re-aligning each beat to a first-pass median by cross-correlation ($\pm20$\,ms), and taking the per-lead median. $99.7\%$ of the 800{,}035 ECGs yield at least three complete beats.

\paragraph{Decoder.} An MLP ($256\to1024\to1024$, ReLU) with a beat head and a rhythm head is trained with squared error on the frozen, standardized embeddings of every ECG in folds 0--17 (718{,}922 ECGs). Its epoch is selected on fold 18 and scores are reported on fold 19. It explains $53\%$ of the variance of the test beats ($R^2=0.532$, against $0.719$ on the features of a randomly initialized encoder: pretraining discards beat detail) and decodes the heart rate with correlation $0.87$. Decoding $\vh_{t+1}$ instead of $\vh_t$ lowers the beat error to the next ECG by $21\%$ ($0.259$ vs.\ $0.329$ on the cross-stay test pairs), so the embeddings do carry the change between visits.

\paragraph{Counterfactual comparison.} Following the headroom tests of the planning analysis (Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}), which compare the true transition with the do-nothing action, we compare the decoded prediction under the true action $\va_t$ with two controls. The \emph{zero action}, $\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\vec 0))$, shows what the state pathway does alone. The \emph{decoy action} $\tilde{\va}_t$ is admissible given $\vy_t$ and has the same numbers of onsets and resolutions as $\va_t$, placed at random on labels that $\va_t$ leaves unchanged. A model that uses \emph{which} labels change should reproduce the observed transition under $\va_t$ and not under $\tilde{\va}_t$.

Beats are compared by shape rather than by squared error, because shrinking the old beat toward the population mean lowers the error without producing the new morphology. Let $\mB_t$ and $\mB_{t+1}$ be the real median beats of $\mX_t$ and $\mX_{t+1}$, and $\rho$ the correlation of per-lead mean-centred beats over the three leads where the beat changes most. The shape score
\[
s(\vb)=\rho(\vb,\mB_{t+1})-\rho(\vb,\mB_t)
\]
is positive when a beat $\vb$ looks more like the new beat than the old one; a flattened copy of $\mB_t$ still scores negative. We keep the active test pairs whose beat changes most (the $10\%$ with the lowest $\rho(\mB_t,\mB_{t+1})$, 410 pairs) and on which the decoder sees both morphologies ($s(\text{Dec}(\vh_t))\le-0.2$ and $s(\text{Dec}(\vh_{t+1}))\ge0.2$). This leaves 179 pairs, ranked by $s(\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\va_t)))$. Leads with fewer than ten distinct amplitude values in either beat are excluded, since z-scoring a nearly flat lead turns its quantization steps into spurious shape changes.

To quantify a counterfactual, we compare the effect of each action relative to the zero action, $\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\va))-\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1}(\vec 0))$. The correlation between the decoy's effect and the true action's is $1$ when the model produces the same change whatever the labels, and should be low for an action-specific model.

\paragraph{Comparison with the zero action.} Figure~\ref{fig:decoder-top} shows the two highest-ranked pairs. In the first, the tall R waves of $\mX_t$ in aVL and V5 give way in $\mX_{t+1}$ to broad negative complexes in III and aVL and a small RS complex in V5; the action includes the onsets of acute myocardial infarction (I21) and of a conduction block (I44). The decoded $\vh_{t+1}$ shows the new complexes and the decoded $\vh_t$ keeps the old R waves. The decoded prediction follows the new morphology as closely as the ceiling: its correlation with $\mB_{t+1}$ and $\mB_t$ is $0.91$ and $-0.01$, against $0.91$ and $0.00$ for the decoded $\vh_{t+1}$. The zero action does not produce this change ($s=-0.33$, against $0.91$ under $\va_t$), so here the action moves the beat. In the second pair, by contrast, the zero action already reproduces the change ($s=0.84$): there it is the state pathway that moves the beat.

Such examples are rare. The decoded prediction looks more like $\mB_{t+1}$ than like $\mB_t$ in 10 of the 179 pairs ($6\%$), and the zero action in 4. In the median pair, the prediction closes $3\%$ of the gap in $s$ between the decoded $\vh_t$ and the decoded $\vh_{t+1}$, and in 53 pairs it moves away from $\mB_{t+1}$. Figure~\ref{fig:decoder-middle-bottom} shows the middle- and last-ranked pairs: the decoded $\vh_{t+1}$ reproduces the new morphology (a tall R wave in V5; a complete QRS inversion in III, aVL and aVF), while the decoded prediction lies on the decoded $\vh_t$. In squared error the prediction still beats do-nothing on $72\%$ of the 179 pairs, by shrinking the old beat rather than changing its shape.

\paragraph{Comparison with a decoy action.} Figures~\ref{fig:decoder-decoy-1} and~\ref{fig:decoder-decoy-2} add the decoy prediction to the four highest-ranked pairs.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{An action-specific case.} In the third pair, the true action (onsets of cardiomyopathy I42, conduction block I44 and heart failure I50) inverts the QRS complexes of aVR, V5 and V6 toward $\mB_{t+1}$. The decoy (onsets I01, I66, I99) stays on the decoded $\vh_t$: its effect is $0.18\times$ the size of the true action's and correlates with it at $0.27$.
\item \textbf{A partly generic case.} In the first pair, the decoy shares no label with $\va_t$ but still reproduces part of the change in III and aVL, with an effect $0.54\times$ the size of the true action's and correlation $0.62$.
\item \textbf{Overall.} On the 2{,}724 active test pairs that admit a decoy, the decoy's effect is as large as the true action's (median ratio $1.08$) and correlates with it at a median of $0.39$ ($0.32$ over the 118 selected pairs that admit one). Among those 118 pairs, the decoy produces the new morphology in $2\%$, against $6\%$ for the true action over all 179.
\end{itemize}
$\text{Dyn}_\phi$ therefore moves the beat by about the same amount whatever labels change, partly along a direction shared by all transitions, with a small label-specific component. These pairs cannot tell whether a decoy's decoded change shows the features its own labels would cause, because no patient undergoes the decoy transition; that would require comparing with the change averaged over the pairs where those labels actually flip. Figure~\ref{fig:decoder-decoy-middle-bottom} repeats the middle and last pairs with the decoy: neither the true action nor the decoy produces the new morphology, and both predictions stay on the decoded $\vh_t$.

\paragraph{Summary.} The decoder recovers the change between visits from $\vh_{t+1}$, but the forward model rarely predicts it. Its improvement over do-nothing in latent space is accounted for by shrinkage toward the mean and does not yield a better decoded ECG. Isolated pairs show an action-driven change of morphology consistent with the transition, but in most pairs the prediction collapses onto the current state, $\text{Dec}(\hat{\vh}_{t+1})\approx\text{Dec}(\vh_t)$, and count-matched decoy actions move it as much as the true one.

\subsection{Encoder Architecture: ResNet vs.\ ViT}
\label{sec:cnn-vit}

The planning analysis (Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}) and the decoder (Appendix~\ref{sec:decoder}) show that $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ does not carry the effect of an action over to held-out transitions, although its training loss keeps falling. This section tests whether that failure is tied to the convolutional encoder. We replace $\text{Enc}_\theta$ with a small transformer and compare the two encoders on memorization of the training pairs, on the quality of their frozen features, and on planning.

\paragraph{Setup.} The transformer is a 1D ViT:
\begin{itemize}
\item Non-overlapping 50\,ms patches (25 samples at 500\,Hz) feed 4 pre-norm blocks of width 128 with 4 attention heads, rotary position embeddings, QK-normalization and SwiGLU MLPs.
\item Its tokens are mean-pooled and mapped linearly to $\vh_t\in\R^{256}$, as the ResNet's output layer does.
\item It has $0.87$M parameters, against $3.98$M for the ResNet; its size matches the ResNet's layers before its last stage ($0.96$M).
\end{itemize}

The two \emph{matched} runs differ only in the encoder. Both train on the multistep cross-stay pool ($k\le12$, Appendix~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix}) with $\lambda=0.05$, weight decay $0.1$, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 1{,}024 and a predictor of hidden width 512, for 100 epochs. Both are read at the final checkpoint, which no validation metric selected.

Secondary runs are not matched to each other and serve to show trends:
\begin{itemize}
\item The matched ResNet run at its best validation loss (epoch 5).
\item A ResNet trained on the multistep pool with $\lambda=0.005$ and weight decay $0.01$ (56 epochs).
\item A ResNet trained on the $k=1$ cross-stay pairs with $\lambda=0.005$ and weight decay $0.01$ (100 epochs).
\item The ViT trained on the $k=1$ cross-stay pairs with the matched recipe for 405 epochs, about as many batches as the multistep runs. It was run with predictor width 512 and with predictor width 128.
\end{itemize}

Randomly initialized ResNet and ViT encoders (seed 0) serve as controls.

\paragraph{Memorization test.} We use membership inference, with members and non-members drawn the same way.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Anchors.} For each patient, one stay $s$ that has a second ECG and a following stay gives three ECGs: $\mX_A$, the first ECG of stay $s$; $\mX_{A'}$, its second ECG; and $\mX_B$, the first ECG of stay $s+1$. The pair $A\to B$ is a $k=1$ cross-stay pair; $\mX_{A'}$ belongs to no cross-stay pair.
\item \textbf{Populations.} Members are 5{,}000 training patients and non-members all 2{,}518 patients of folds 18--19. Each patient contributes one anchor, so observations are independent.
\item \textbf{Membership is verified per checkpoint.} Every member's $A\to B$ pair is in the checkpoint's own training pairs, and no $\mX_{A'}$ and no non-member pair is.
\end{itemize}

Each test assigns one score per patient and reports the AUROC separating members from non-members, with DeLong 95\% confidence intervals. Chance is $0.5$, and the random-init encoders, which have no members, measure the floor. Distances are taken relative to the mean distance to other patients' $\vh_B$ in the same population.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Pair:} closeness of $\vh_A$ to $\vh_B$. A high score means the recording and/or the patient is memorized.
\item \textbf{Patient:} closeness of the never-trained $\vh_{A'}$ to $\vh_B$. A high score means the patient is memorized.
\item \textbf{Recording:} the pair score relative to the patient score. A high score means the trained ECG sits closer to $\vh_B$ than an untrained ECG of the same stay, so the specific recording is memorized.
\item \textbf{Action:} on pairs with $\va_t\neq\vec 0$, the share of the prediction error removed by the true action, $1-E(\va_t)/E(\vec 0)$, where $E(\va)=\lVert\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_A,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va))-\vh_B\rVert^2$. A high score means the action effects of the training transitions are memorized in $\text{Dyn}_\phi$.
\end{itemize}

  \end{itemize}

  \begin{itemize}
  \item On the multistep pool, the pair test rises from $0.867$ for ECGs in one training pair per epoch to $0.984$ for ECGs in $12.3$.

\item \textbf{The ViT encoder stays clean even under heavy repetition.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item After 405 epochs on $k=1$ pairs, in which each training pair is seen 405 times, its pair test is $0.518$.
  \item The only trace is in the most exposed patients of the multistep run, those with at least 9 stays: $0.566$ $[0.531$--$0.601]$.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The ViT's forward model memorizes instead.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item The action test is $0.641$, as high as the ResNet's $0.633$. It reaches $0.832$ after 405 epochs on $k=1$ pairs, and a $3.8\times$ smaller predictor lowers it only to $0.766$.

  \item The smaller encoder moves the memorization into $\text{Dyn}_\phi$; it does not remove it.
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item the 8{,}062 test pairs of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning} (fold 19);
\item the 8{,}271 pairs of training fold 17, which are in every checkpoint's training pairs (verified).
\end{itemize}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CEM.} Ranking by $E$ alone, with the search and soft readout of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}.
\item \textbf{Decoys.} No search: the true action is ranked against two kinds of admissible decoy. \emph{Same-count} decoys are 16 actions with the true numbers of onsets and resolutions on random labels. The \emph{zero} decoy is the do-nothing action. The score is a within-pair AUROC, with chance at $0.5$.
\item \textbf{Per-label readout.} $\delta_i=E(\vec 0)-E(\ve_i)$ for the single admissible flip $\ve_i$ of label $i$, and $p_i=\sigma(\text{logit}\,\pi_i+\delta_i/\tau)$, combined as in the contextualized score. The temperature $\tau$ is chosen on fold 18. This is the readout anticipated at the end of Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}.
\end{itemize}
Each decoy and per-label score is repeated with $\vh_t$ as the target in place of $\vh_{t+1}$ (``nothing changed''). This keeps the patient and removes the change, so what survives is read from $\vh_t$, not from the outcome ECG. Intervals come from a patient-level bootstrap. \paragraph{Planning results} (Table~\ref{tab:cnn-vit-planning}).
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{CEM fails with either encoder.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item On test, the soft readout scores $0.470$ for the ResNet and $0.519$ for the ViT, and $0.435$--$0.563$ across all checkpoints, against $0.750$ for carry-forward.

\item \textbf{On held-out pairs, the energy carries almost no evidence about the transition.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Against same-count decoys, the true action scores $0.524$ for the ResNet ($0.484$ when nothing changed) and $0.563$ for the ViT ($0.563$ when nothing changed). The ViT's higher score therefore comes from $\vh_t$ alone.
  \item The ViTs' true action is \emph{worse} than doing nothing on held-out pairs ($0.346$--$0.397$): their action effects move the prediction away from $\vh_{t+1}$.
  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{On training pairs, the energy carries strong evidence with either encoder.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item Against same-count decoys, the two matched models score $0.722$ and $0.723$. After 100--405 epochs on $k=1$ pairs, the ResNet reaches $0.890$ and the ViT $0.849$.

  \end{itemize}
\item \textbf{The per-label readout beats carry-forward, but mostly through $\vh_t$.}
  \begin{itemize}
  \item It scores $0.755$--$0.800$ on test, against $0.750$ for carry-forward.
  \item Scored against $\vh_t$ alone, it already reaches $0.740$--$0.782$. The outcome ECG adds at most $0.021$, and the same for both architectures: $+0.020$ each, paired difference $+0.000$ $[-0.023, +0.026]$.
  \item The validation AUROC is flat from about $3\times$ the spread of $\delta$ upward, which is the limit in which $E$ only breaks ties within each label's present and absent groups. Letting $E$ override the carry-forward ordering lowers it.
  \item The best readouts come from the least memorized forward models, the ResNet at epoch 5 ($0.800$) and Dynamics (Standard) ($0.790$). Both remain below the Inverse Dynamics model ($0.827$).
  \end{itemize}
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Summary.}
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Encoder memorization.} Replacing the ResNet with the 4.6$\times$ smaller ViT removes the encoder-level memorization: trained pairs no longer collapse, and the dependence on exposure disappears.
\item \textbf{Forward-model memorization.} The swap does not make $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ generalize. The ViT's forward model memorizes the action effects of its training transitions as much as the ResNet's does.
\item \textbf{Frozen features.} The ViT does not give better features: the two encoders tie at $\vh$, and the ViT's middle-layer ceiling is lower.
\item \textbf{Planning.} Planning is unchanged. With either encoder, CEM is exploited, and the energy separates the true transition only on training pairs. A per-label readout gains about $0.02$ over carry-forward from the outcome ECG, the same for both. The planning failure lies in how the forward model generalizes, not in the encoder's collapse.
\item \textbf{Caveats.} The comparison does not yet separate architecture from capacity, since a ResNet of about 1M parameters or a ViT of about 4M has not been trained. Each configuration has one seed.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pretraining Without Actions}
\label{sec:no-actions}

The forward objective conditions $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ on the transition $\va_t$, which is built from the labels. The preceding sections show that $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ does not carry the action's effect over to held-out pairs. This section asks whether the conditioning matters for the encoder at all. We retrain with every action set to $\vec 0$ and compare the finetuned encoders on MIMIC triage classification.

\paragraph{Setup.} We set $\va_t=\vec 0$ for every pair, in training and in validation. Because $\text{Proj}_\omega$ is an MLP with biases, $\text{Proj}_\omega(\vec 0)$ becomes a learned constant that the first layer of $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ absorbs as a bias. The model is therefore $\hat\vh_{t+1}=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t)$, with the same encoder, predictor capacity, SIGReg term and optimizer, and the first layer of $\text{Proj}_\omega$ receives no gradient. The objective no longer uses labels: it predicts the embedding of the patient's next stay from the current one. On the $k=1$ cross-stay pairs used here, this removes the action from the $51.7\%$ of training pairs that carry one, which change $2.48$ labels on average (Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats}). On the pooled within- and cross-stay pairs of Dynamics (Standard), only $25.0\%$ of pairs would be affected, since within-stay pairs are almost all stable.

Both runs use the ViT encoder of Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit} with its $k=1$ recipe: cross-stay pairs, $\lambda=0.05$, weight decay $0.1$, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size 1{,}024, predictor width 512, seed 42 and 405 epochs. They differ only in $\va_t$. The action-conditioned run is the ``ViT, $k=1$, 405 epochs'' run of Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit}, and we also include its predictor-width-128 variant as a second action-conditioned run. Each run is read at its highest online linear-probe AUROC on validation, which falls at epoch 370 without actions and at epochs 402 and 403 with them.

Each encoder is then finetuned end to end with a linear head for the 76 labels on the 261{,}496 first-of-stay ECGs of the training folds, with learning rate $10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-2}$ and batch size 1{,}024. Training stops early on the loss over the 14{,}456 first-of-stay ECGs of fold 18 (patience 20), and we keep the checkpoint with the lowest loss (epochs 39, 57 and 57). The test set is the 14{,}237 first-of-stay ECGs of fold 19, scored as in Table~\ref{tab:classification}: macro-AUROC is the mean over 1{,}000 bootstrap resamples, reported with a 95\% interval.

Two quantities logged on the 8{,}244 validation pairs during pretraining complement the test. The \emph{error ratio} is the prediction error $\lVert\hat\vh_{t+1}-\vh_{t+1}\rVert^2$ relative to that of doing nothing, $\lVert\vh_t-\vh_{t+1}\rVert^2$, averaged over pairs. The prediction uses $\va=\vec 0$ without actions and the true $\va_t$ with them, and each model is measured in its own embedding space. The \emph{true vs.\ zero} score is the within-pair AUROC of Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit} of the true action against the zero action, on pairs with a change, under the action-conditioned model's own energy $E$. Chance is $0.5$, and a score below it means the zero action predicts $\vh_{t+1}$ better than the true one.

\paragraph{Results.} Removing the actions leaves finetuned classification unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:no-actions}). The encoder pretrained without actions scores $0.7391$ $[0.7266$--$0.7529]$, against $0.7409$ $[0.7273$--$0.7557]$ for the matched action-conditioned encoder. The gap of $0.0018$ is about an eighth of either interval's half-width, and the three ViT encoders together span only $0.005$, inside every interval.

The action does not improve prediction on held-out pairs either. Within each action-conditioned model, the zero action predicts $\vh_{t+1}$ better than the true action on most validation pairs with a change, with within-pair AUROCs of $0.358$ and $0.376$. Across models, the action-free one has the lowest error ratio, $0.941$ against $0.992$ for the matched action-conditioned model. This agrees with Appendix~\ref{sec:cnn-vit}, where $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ fits the action effects of its training transitions but these effects do not hold on held-out ones. Nor does the online probe favor the actions: it reads $0.633$ without them and $0.620$ with them, while the predictor-width-128 run reads $0.642$. Its ordering therefore does not follow the conditioning, and it also differs from the finetuned ordering.

The ResNet rows of Table~\ref{tab:no-actions} serve only as orientation. All three ViT encoders fall below Dynamics (Standard) ($0.7500$) and above Inverse Dynamics ($0.7373$), but those two use a ResNet with other pair sets and other $\lambda$. They were also finetuned on all training ECGs, including the 298{,}869 recorded outside any stay, whose labels are all negative. Only the ViT rows compare matched runs.

\paragraph{Summary.} With the ViT encoder on $k=1$ cross-stay pairs, conditioning on the diagnosis transition brings no measurable benefit to the encoder. A label-free predictor of the next stay's embedding gives the same finetuned triage AUROC and, on held-out pairs, predicts its next-stay embedding at least as well. Whatever the forward objective contributes to the encoder therefore comes from predicting the patient's next ECG, not from the labels in $\va_t$. Three caveats apply. Each configuration has one seed and the intervals are per model, so a paired bootstrap on shared test resamples and more seeds would bound the effect more tightly. The ResNet recipe of Dynamics (Standard), in which only $25.0\%$ of training pairs carry an action, has not been ablated. Finally, labels still select the pretraining checkpoint through the online probe, in both arms alike.
