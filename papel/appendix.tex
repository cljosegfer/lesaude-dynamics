\subsection{Pairing system}
\label{sec:pairing-appendix}

Longitudinal pairs $(\mX_t, \mX_{t+1})$ for pretraining are constructed directly from MIMIC-IV-ECG's hospital-stay structure, using \texttt{ecg\_no\_within\_stay} (enws) as the per-admission ECG counter and \texttt{subject\_id} to group all ECGs belonging to the same patient, time-ordered by \texttt{ecg\_time}. Two pair types are distinguished:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Within-stay}: consecutive ECGs recorded during the same hospital stay, i.e.\ enws increasing between two chronologically adjacent records for a patient. Since ICD-10 diagnosis codes in this dataset are assigned at the hospitalization level (one code set per admission), $\vy_t = \vy_{t+1}$ for essentially every within-stay pair; the transition $\va_t = \text{clip}(\vy_{t+1}-\vy_t,-1,1)$ is almost always the zero vector, so this pair type contributes encoder training signal from waveform variability alone, not from a genuine diagnostic transition.
\item \textbf{Cross-stay}: the first ECG of stay $N$ paired with the first ECG of stay $N+1$ for the same patient (enws $=0$ on both sides), i.e.\ pairing across separate admissions rather than within one. Because separate hospitalizations frequently carry different discharge diagnoses, this is where the pathology-transition signal $\va_t \ne \vec{0}$ actually concentrates.
\end{itemize}

Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats} reports, on the training split (folds 0--17, 721{,}002 exams), the pair count, the stable/active split (stable: $\va_t=\vec{0}$; active: $\lVert\va_t\rVert_0>0$), and the mean number of flipped ICD-10 columns among active pairs, for each pair type. As expected from the per-admission label assignment, within-stay pairs are stable in 160{,}371/160{,}400 ($99.98\%$) of cases; cross-stay pairs are close to evenly split (72{,}386 stable / 77{,}537 active), confirming that admission-to-admission transitions are the primary source of diagnostic-change supervision for the dynamics objective.

\paragraph{Multistep cross-stay pairing.} The cross-stay pairing above only links stay $N$ to stay $N+1$ (hop distance $k=1$). We additionally explore pairing stay $N$ to stay $N+k$ for growing $k$, pooling all hops up to $k$ into a single, larger cross-stay pair set (all such pairs stay within one patient, so pooling introduces no fold leakage). For a patient with $L$ recorded stays, hop $k$ contributes $\max(0, L-k)$ pairs; across the 111{,}573 training patients with at least one stay (mean 2.34, median 1, max 125 stays per patient), $k=1$ alone reproduces the 149{,}923-pair count of Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats} exactly, which we use as a consistency check between the two pair-construction scripts.

Table~\ref{tab:multistep-offsets} reports, for each hop $k$, the pairs contributed at that hop alone, the cumulative pair count pooling hops $1,\dots,k$, the resulting cumulative stable/active split, and the percentage growth in pool size relative to the current $k=1$-only scheme. Pooling grows the training pool quickly at first (already $+66.9\%$ by $k=2$) and continues growing, if with diminishing returns, out to $k=20$ ($+358.0\%$, i.e.\ a $4.6\times$ larger pool than $k=1$ alone); the active fraction of the cumulative pool rises alongside it, from $51.7\%$ at $k=1$ to $74.0\%$ at $k=20$, since more distant admission pairs are, if anything, slightly more likely to carry a genuine diagnostic change than adjacent ones.

\subsection{Pairing Scheme Ablation: Pretraining Dynamics}
\label{sec:pairing-ablation}

We compare three pretraining configurations of the forward Dynamics model (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}), differing only in which pairs the SSL objective is trained on: (i) the pooled baseline, using both within-stay and cross-stay pairs from the traditional pair index ($k=1$ only); (ii) cross-stay-only, restricting the same traditional pair index to cross-stay pairs; and (iii) multistep cross-stay, pooling cross-stay pairs at every hop $k=1,\dots,12$ (Section~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix}). All three share the same encoder architecture, batch size, and optimizer configuration; only \texttt{pairs\_path} and \texttt{pair\_types} differ.

\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{papel/fig/pairing_ablation_losses.png}
\caption{Validation loss curves for the three pairing configurations of Section~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation}: SIGReg regularization loss (left), dynamics prediction loss (middle), and total loss (right), against training step.}
\label{fig:pairing-ablation-losses}
\end{figure}

Figure~\ref{fig:pairing-ablation-losses} shows two effects not anticipated from encoder architecture alone. First, cross-stay-only pretraining reaches a visibly higher validation \texttt{pred\_loss} than the pooled baseline, consistent with training on substantially fewer pairs under the same epoch budget: pooling within-stay and cross-stay pairs yields 310{,}323 training pairs (Table~\ref{tab:pairing-stats}), versus 149{,}923 for cross-stay alone, so the restricted run sees under half the unique pairs per epoch and overfits sooner despite within-stay pairs being label-stable ($99.98\%$) and thus contributing mostly waveform-level, rather than transition-level, training signal. Second, the multistep run's \texttt{reg\_loss} sits roughly $5\times$ higher than the baseline's. The SIGReg statistic (Appendix~\ref{sec:sigreg} % TODO: fix ref if the SIGReg formula is documented under a different label
) is a sliced Epps--Pulley goodness-of-fit statistic that is $O(1)$ under a well-matched (near-Gaussian) embedding distribution but scales linearly with the effective batch size used in its estimator under any sustained deviation from it; since $\lambda$ was calibrated against the baseline configuration's regularizer scale, a shift in that scale — whether from genuinely harder-to-regularize embeddings under the larger, more heterogeneous multistep pair pool, or from an unpinned world-size/batch-scale confound across runs — changes the effective SSL/regularization balance without $\lambda$ itself having been retuned. %TODO: confirm via train/loss_ratio logs and matched world_size across runs before drawing a final conclusion here; update this paragraph once confirmed.

\subsection{Embedding-Space Diagnostic}
\label{sec:embedding-diagnostic}

To probe what each of the three pretrained Dynamics checkpoints from Section~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation} has actually learned beyond the pretraining loss curves, we run a post-hoc diagnostic on a random batch of validation pairs. For each pair, we compute the actual current- and next-state embeddings $\vh_t=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_t)$, $\vh_{t+1}=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_{t+1})$, and three candidate predictions of $\vh_{t+1}$ from $\vh_t$: $\hat{\vh}_{t+1}=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ using the pair's true transition; a zero-action baseline $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\vec{0}))$; and a noise-action baseline $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\tilde{\va}_t))$ using a synthetic ternary vector $\tilde{\va}_t$ magnitude-matched to the pair's own true action — $\lVert\tilde{\va}_t\rVert_0=\lVert\va_t\rVert_0$, with flips placed on uniformly random label positions and random $\pm1$ signs — i.e.\ ``right sparsity, wrong labels,'' isolating whether $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ uses \emph{which} labels changed rather than merely how many. We score all three against $\vh_{t+1}$ by MSE and by cosine similarity, both broken down by transition magnitude $\lVert\va_t\rVert_0$ (the number of ICD-10 columns that flip) into all pairs, active pairs ($\lVert\va_t\rVert_0>0$), and stable pairs ($\va_t=\vec{0}$), to test whether each checkpoint's predictions specifically track the size of the diagnostic change rather than being uniformly accurate or uniformly insensitive to $\va_t$.

\subsection{Displacement-Conditioned Dynamics Variant}
\label{sec:displacement-diagnostic}

The Inverse Dynamics model (Section~\ref{sec:inverse}) is parameterized in terms of the latent displacement $\vd_t = \vh_{t+1}-\vh_t$ rather than the absolute next state. We implement the analogous reparameterization for the forward Dynamics model: $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ is trained to output a predicted displacement $\hat{\vd}_t=\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ rather than a predicted next state, and the prediction term of the loss is computed directly against the true displacement,
\[
\mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda)\,\text{MSE}\big(\vh_{t+1}-\vh_t,\ \text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))\big) + \lambda\,\big(\text{SIGReg}(H_t)+\text{SIGReg}(H_{t+1})\big),
\]
with the architecture of $\text{Dyn}_\phi$, the SIGReg regularization, and all other hyperparameters (batch size, optimizer, $\lambda$, learning rate) unchanged from the standard next-state formulation of Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}, so that the two variants differ only in what the predictor's output is trained to represent.

To probe this variant with the same methodology as Section~\ref{sec:embedding-diagnostic}, we reuse its do-nothing / true-action / zero-action / noise-action comparison, reconstructing a next-state prediction for each condition as $\vh_t+\hat{\vd}_t$ so that all four columns of Table~\ref{tab:embedding-mse-displacement} remain directly comparable, in units of MSE to $\vh_{t+1}$, to the corresponding columns of Table~\ref{tab:embedding-mse}.

\subsection{CEM Planning with the Forward Dynamics Model}
\label{sec:cem-planning}

This section evaluates the Planning procedure of Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}, in which diagnosis is recovered by searching for the transition $\va^\star$ that the forward model $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ judges to best explain an observed pair of embeddings. We report it in full because the outcome is negative, and because the way it fails is informative about what the forward model has, and has not, learned.

\paragraph{Setup.} The search follows Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}: two Bernoulli heads, onset and resolution, masked by the known previous label $\vy_{t-1}$ so that only admissible transitions are proposed. Both are initialized at Laplace-smoothed per-class base rates estimated on the 149{,}923 training cross-stay pairs. The onset rate has mean $0.0104$ and median $0.0028$; the resolution rate has mean $0.476$ and median $0.476$. Each CEM run uses a population of $N=512$ candidates, $K=64$ elites and $16$ iterations. The elite refit keeps weight $0.1$ on the previous distribution. Every Bernoulli parameter is clamped to $[0.005, 0.995]$ so that rare onsets remain explorable. The do-nothing action seeds the incumbent, so $E(\va^\star)\le E(\vec 0)$ by construction. We verified the search on a synthetic oracle energy $E(\va)=\lVert\va-\va_{\text{gt}}\rVert^2$: it recovers the true action exactly for every pair, and all $2.1$M sampled candidates were admissible.

We evaluate all seven forward checkpoints: the three pairing configurations of Section~\ref{sec:pairing-ablation}, the displacement variant of Section~\ref{sec:displacement-diagnostic}, and, for the three next-state configurations, the twin checkpoint selected by the online linear-probe AUROC rather than by validation loss. Evaluation uses exactly the protocol of the inverse model and the carry-forward baseline: the 8{,}062 cross-stay test pairs ($k=1$, fold 19), macro AUROC over classes with both outcomes present, and 1{,}000 bootstrap resamples with a fixed seed. Onset and resolution AUROCs are computed only over the labels where each head is live ($\vy_{t-1,i}=0$ and $\vy_{t-1,i}=1$ respectively). Scoring them over all entries would credit the planner with the structural knowledge that a present label cannot have an onset.

\paragraph{Reference numbers.} Recomputed on the current version of the pair index, the carry-forward baseline $\hat{\vy}_t=\vy_{t-1}$ reaches $0.7515$ $[0.7361$--$0.7682]$ and the inverse dynamics model reaches $0.8286$ $[0.8124$--$0.8456]$ contextualized macro AUROC.
The inverse model's (unmasked) onset and resolution AUROCs are $0.6591$ and $0.6958$.

\paragraph{Minimizing reconstruction error alone.} Table~\ref{tab:cem-test} reports the planner with candidates ranked by $E(\va)$ alone. For every checkpoint, the contextualized AUROC falls far below carry-forward. The soft readout is at or near chance ($0.45$--$0.55$), and the hard readout reaches only $0.57$--$0.61$.

The diagnostics isolate the cause in the scoring function, not in the search:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{The true action carries no signal.} The ground-truth transition beats the do-nothing action in only $47$--$54\%$ of active pairs, where chance is $50\%$. When it does, the mean reduction in $E$ is at most $0.005$ (Table~\ref{tab:cem-test}). This reproduces, on the full test set and for all seven checkpoints, the earlier finding on a single checkpoint that raw reconstruction error does not favour the true action on held-out data.
\item \textbf{The search exploits the model.} CEM lowers $E$ by $40$--$75\%$ relative to the do-nothing action. It does so with $6$--$12$ onsets per pair, against $0.67$ in the ground truth, and onset precision stays below $2\%$.
\item \textbf{Every active pair is a model failure.} In every active pair, the returned action scores at least as well as the true one, and it is never the true one. The search is not missing the true action; the model assigns lower error to transitions that never occur.
\end{itemize}
In other words, $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ is \emph{controllable} but not \emph{identifiable}: the $2^C$ admissible action codes, most far outside the training distribution of $\approx 2.5$ flips per active pair, span enough of the displacement space to fit a large part of $\vh_t-\vh_{t-1}$, while the one realistic code, the true transition, fits no better than doing nothing.

\paragraph{Prior-regularized objective.} Ranking candidates by $F(\va)=E(\va)/\tau+\sum_{i:\va_i\ne0}\log\frac{1-\pi_i}{\pi_i}$ instead (Section~\ref{sec:dynamics}) makes every flip pay its prior log-odds: about $4.6$--$5.9$ nats for a typical onset, and close to zero, or negative, for a resolution. We swept $\tau\in\{3{\cdot}10^{-4},\dots,1\}$ on the validation split (8{,}244 cross-stay pairs, 100 bootstrap resamples) for all seven checkpoints (Table~\ref{tab:cem-tau}).

The prior removes the exploitation: onsets per pair fall from $8$--$12$ at $\tau\to0$ to $\approx0.3$ at $\tau=10^{-2}$, and to zero for $\tau\ge0.3$. The best soft AUROC improves accordingly, from $0.46$--$0.54$ to $0.57$--$0.59$, but it remains far below validation carry-forward ($0.758$). The hard readout peaks at $0.675$. At no temperature do the masked onset or resolution AUROCs leave the $0.50$--$0.53$ band, so the regularization suppresses spurious transitions without uncovering a real signal. This is consistent with the headroom test above, where the true transition does not lower $E$ in the first place.

\paragraph{Why the soft readout falls short of carry-forward even at large $\tau$.} As $\tau\to\infty$, $F$ reduces to the prior, whose posterior marginals are exactly the base rates. Reading those marginals through the contextualized combination gives $\vy_{t-1,i}(1-\pi^{\text{res}}_i)+(1-\vy_{t-1,i})\pi^{\text{ons}}_i$, a score that preserves the carry-forward ordering and so matches its AUROC.

CEM's final head parameters are not posterior marginals, however. The elite refit concentrates each Bernoulli on the mode, so every label whose resolution rate exceeds $0.5$ collapses to $p^{\text{res}}_i\approx1$. Its persistence score then falls to $\approx0.005$, tied with the onset scores of absent labels, and the carry-forward ordering is lost ($0.58$ instead of $0.758$). The hard readout suffers the same loss for the same reason: resolving every label whose prior favours resolution scores $0.669$.

A readout based on per-label conditional posteriors around the returned action, $p_i=\sigma\big(F(\va^\star_{i\to0})-F(\va^\star_{i\to\text{flip}})\big)$, would recover carry-forward in the prior limit and add the model's evidence at finite $\tau$. We leave it to future work. Given the headroom results above, we expect it to match, not exceed, carry-forward with the current checkpoints.

\paragraph{Summary.} With the present forward models, CEM planning does not recover diagnostic transitions. Minimizing reconstruction error alone is exploited through out-of-distribution action combinations, and adding the prior removes the exploitation without uncovering signal, because the ground-truth transition is no better than doing nothing under $\text{Dyn}_\phi$. The inverse dynamics model, trained to discriminate transitions directly, reaches $0.8286$ on the same pairs.

Making the forward model usable as a planner likely requires a training objective that explicitly penalizes wrong-action predictions, for example a contrastive term separating $\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t))$ from predictions under decoy actions. Besides the posterior-marginal readout, this is the main direction we leave for future work.

\subsection{Joint Forward and Inverse Pretraining}
\label{sec:joint-pretraining}

The two world-model components have complementary strengths. The forward Dynamics encoder transfers best under finetuning (Table~\ref{tab:classification}), but its forward model cannot be used for planning (Appendix~\ref{sec:cem-planning}). The Inverse Dynamics model gives the best contextualized inference (Table~\ref{tab:contextualized}), with a weaker encoder. This section tests whether a single encoder trained under both objectives obtains both.

\paragraph{Setup.} The forward predictor $\text{Dyn}_\phi$ (with its projector $\text{Proj}_\omega$) and the inverse predictor $\text{Inv}_\psi$ share the encoder $\text{Enc}_\theta$ and nothing else; there is no coupling term between them. With $\vh_t=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_t)$ and $\vh_{t+1}=\text{Enc}_\theta(\mX_{t+1})$, the loss adds the prediction terms of Sections~\ref{sec:dynamics} and~\ref{sec:inverse} with equal weight,
\[
\mathcal{L} = (1-\lambda)\Big(\text{MSE}\big(\text{Dyn}_\phi(\vh_t,\text{Proj}_\omega(\va_t)),\,\vh_{t+1}\big) + \text{BCE}^{\text{ons}}_t + \text{BCE}^{\text{res}}_t\Big) + \lambda\big(\text{SIGReg}(\mH_t)+\text{SIGReg}(\mH_{t+1})\big),
\]
where $\text{BCE}^{\text{ons}}_t$ and $\text{BCE}^{\text{res}}_t$ are the onset and resolution terms of $\text{Inv}_\psi(\vh_{t+1}-\vh_t)$. Both terms are computed on every pair of the multistep cross-stay pool ($k\le12$, 605{,}533 training pairs; Appendix~\ref{sec:pairing-appendix}).

Training was interrupted after 35 epochs; every selection criterion reported below had peaked by epoch 22. Epochs are counted from 0. Downstream results use the checkpoint with the best validation contextualized AUROC, reached at epoch 4, under exactly the protocols and test sets of Sections~\ref{sec:contextualized}, \ref{sec:classification} and~\ref{sec:distshift}.

\paragraph{Results.} Table~\ref{tab:joint-results} compares the joint encoder with the single-objective models.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Contextualized inference is retained.} The joint model reaches $0.8264$, within the confidence interval of the Inverse Dynamics model ($0.8286$) and $7.5$ points above carry-forward.
\item \textbf{Transfer outside MIMIC is mostly retained.} Finetuned on the external datasets, the joint encoder lies between the two single-objective encoders.
